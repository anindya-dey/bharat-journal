<p>
  India's solar story is one of the fastest energy transitions ever recorded.
  In 2010 the country had <strong>161 MW</strong> of solar; by 2026 it had
  built more than <strong>150 GW</strong> — most of it in the last decade, and
  at some of the lowest tariffs in the world. Wind has grown steadily alongside
  it, and together with hydro, biomass and small hydro, renewables now supply
  <strong>over half</strong> of India's installed power capacity.
</p>

<p>
  The design choices mattered. Solar parks aggregated land and transmission;
  reverse auctions drove tariffs down to world-leading levels; and the
  <strong>International Solar Alliance</strong>, co-founded by India, turned a
  domestic programme into global leadership. This is the foundation the next
  energy bets — green hydrogen, round-the-clock renewables, and the data-centre
  grid — all build on.
</p>
