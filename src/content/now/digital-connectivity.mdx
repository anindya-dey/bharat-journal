<p>
  India's first mobile call was made in <strong>1995</strong>, the same year
  public internet arrived — and teledensity was under 1 per 100. Three decades
  later, nearly <strong>80 in every 100</strong> people have a mobile
  subscription and <strong>70%</strong> are online. The mobile explosion of the
  2000s, driven by falling call rates and competition, is what made the
  smartphone the default computer for a billion people — and what made UPI,
  digital welfare and remote work possible.
</p>

<p>
  The build-out keeps extending: [BharatNet](https://www.bbnl.nic.in/)'s fibre to villages,
  5G rollout, and cheap data (among the lowest per-GB prices in the world)
  mean the connectivity layer is now deep enough to carry the next generation
  of services.
</p>
