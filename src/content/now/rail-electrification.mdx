export const electrified = rail.series
  .filter((d) => d.year !== 2014)
  .map((d) => ({

    date: `${d.year}-01-01`,
    value: d.pct,
  }));

<p>
  Indian Railways ran its first electric train in 1925 — Bombay suburban,
  1,500 V DC. The modern system, 25 kV AC, was adopted in 1957, and by 1991
  still covered barely 16% of the route. For four decades, electrification
  ambled along at roughly <strong>340 km a year</strong>, the pace of a system
  that treated it as a maintenance project. Then, in the mid-2010s, the
  ambition changed: complete it. Between 2014 and 2025 the pace averaged
  <strong>~4,350 km a year</strong>, and by 2025 roughly <strong>99.2%</strong>
  of the ~70,000 km route was electrified — one of the largest
  single-infrastructure decarbonisation programmes in the world.
</p>

<p>
  The story the full series tells is the point of this journal: the 2025
  number is not a 2014 invention. The first electric trains, the AC traction
  standard, the suburban systems of Bombay, Madras and Calcutta, the CORE
  electrification organisation (1979) — all pre-date it. What changed was
  <em>pace</em>, driven by a simple piece of arithmetic: electric traction
  costs the Railways roughly a third of diesel per 1,000 GTKM, and every
  electrified km also serves the Dedicated Freight Corridors now operating
  alongside it (~2,741 km by March 2025). The curve bends where strategy
  meets arithmetic.
</p>

<p>
  A note on method: electrification year-points are reported by decade
  snapshots (1951, 1961, 1971 …), not annually — each year in the chart is an
  official reported figure, interpolated smoothly between them. The 2014
  point (~33%) is interpolated from reported FY14 figures and is flagged in
  the dataset.
</p>
