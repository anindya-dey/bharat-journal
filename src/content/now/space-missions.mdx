<p>
  India's space programme is the country's signature move: doing more with less. Its first rocket, in 1963, was carried to the
  launchpad on a bicycle. Six decades later, Chandrayaan-3 made the first-ever
  soft landing near the Moon's south pole — for less than the cost of many
  feature films.
</p>

<p>
  It goes beyond prestige. ISRO has launched
  <strong>434 foreign satellites</strong> for 34 countries, flown
  <strong>136 spacecraft missions</strong>, and built the navigation
  (NavIC), communication and earth-observation systems that underpin daily life
  in India. With the sector opened to private players in 2020, a commercial
  space economy is now taking shape around it.
</p>
