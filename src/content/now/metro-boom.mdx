<p>
  India's first metro — Kolkata's — opened in <strong>1984</strong>, and for
  nearly two decades it stood almost alone. Then came Delhi Metro in 2002,
  delivered on time and within budget, and the template caught on. Bengaluru,
  Chennai, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Kochi, Lucknow, Jaipur, Nagpur, Kanpur, Pune,
  Agra, and more followed. Today <strong>over twenty</strong> Indian cities run
  a metro or rapid-transit system, with <strong>~1,070 km</strong> of
  operational route — one of the largest urban-rail build-outs anywhere, and
  still accelerating.
</p>

<p>
  Metros are the visible layer of a deeper change: cities that once moved
  almost entirely by private vehicle and bus now have a high-capacity spine,
  and the next wave — regional rapid transit like Namo Bharat — is beginning
  to link the metros to their surrounding regions.
</p>
