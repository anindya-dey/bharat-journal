<p>
  When PM Jan Dhan Yojana launched on 28 August 2014, it opened 15 million
  accounts on day one and 18.1 million in the first week — a Guinness World
  Record. A decade on, the numbers tell their own story: over
  <strong>55 crore accounts</strong> (550+ million), average balances up from
  ₹1,279 (2015) to ~₹4,700, and deposits above ₹2.6 lakh crore.
</p>

<p>
  Two design choices turned this into infrastructure. First,
  accounts came bundled with a RuPay debit card and accident insurance, making
  the account <em>useful</em> from week one. Second, the account became the
  delivery endpoint for welfare: paired with [Aadhaar](https://uidai.gov.in/) and mobile numbers — the
  JAM trinity — it carried subsidies straight to beneficiaries, replacing the
  long chains of the past. The World Bank estimates direct benefit transfer
  saved the government roughly <strong>₹3.48 lakh crore</strong> by March
  2023, and credits the JAM stack with helping move ~171 million people out of
  extreme poverty between 2011 and 2023.
</p>

<p>
  The Findex bar shows it plainly: between 2014 and 2017 India moved from
  53% to 80% of adults having a bank account — closing, in three years, a gap
  that most countries take generations to close. And the 2021 dip to 78%
  reflects a definitional change (mobile-money accounts counted differently),
  not people losing accounts.
</p>

<p class="text-sm text-fog italic">
  Yearly snapshots are as of August each year (Ministry of Finance reporting).
  2014 point reflects the launch week record; the first full-year snapshot is
  Aug 2015. The 2025 point combines PMJDY dashboard totals with World Bank
  reporting.
</p>
