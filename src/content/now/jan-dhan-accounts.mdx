<p>
  When PM Jan Dhan Yojana launched on 28 August 2014, the questions were
  louder than the expectations: 15 million accounts on day one, 18.1 million in
  the first week — a Guinness World Record, and a wave of scepticism about
  zero-balance accounts being opened for the numbers. A decade on, the record
  answers differently: over <strong>55 crore accounts</strong> (550+ million),
  average balances up from ₹1,279 (2015) to ~₹4,700, and deposits above
  ₹2.6 lakh crore.
</p>

<p>
  Two design choices turned a bank-account drive into infrastructure. First,
  accounts came bundled with a RuPay debit card and accident insurance, making
  the account <em>useful</em> from week one rather than a compliance artefact.
  Second, the account became the delivery endpoint for welfare: paired with
  Aadhaar and mobile numbers — the JAM trinity — it let subsidies flow directly
  to beneficiaries, bypassing the leaky pipelines of the past. The World Bank
  estimates DBT saved the government roughly <strong>₹3.48 lakh crore</strong>
  in leakages by March 2023, and credits the JAM stack with helping move
  ~171 million people out of extreme poverty between 2011 and 2023.
</p>

<p>
  The deeper story the Findex bar shows: between 2014 and 2017 India moved from
  53% to 80% of adults having a bank account — closing, in three years, a gap
  that most countries take generations to close. And the 2021 dip to 78%
  reflects a definitional change (mobile-money accounts counted differently),
  not people losing accounts.
</p>

<p class="text-sm text-fog italic">
  Yearly snapshots are as of August each year (Ministry of Finance reporting).
  2014 point reflects the launch week record; the first full-year snapshot is
  Aug 2015. The 2025 point combines PMJDY dashboard totals with World Bank
  reporting.
</p>
