<p>
  In 1872, roughly <strong>3%</strong> of India could read. At independence the
  figure was about <strong>18%</strong>. By the 2011 census it had reached
  <strong>74%</strong> — and the gender gap, once enormous, has been narrowing
  with each decade. Universal elementary education became a national mission
  through <strong>Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan</strong> (2001) and a fundamental right
  through the <strong>Right to Education Act</strong> (2009).
</p>

<p>
  The next chapter is quality and access in the mother tongue — and that is
  where India's language technology and digital rails can do something no
  previous generation could: put a patient, personal tutor within reach of
  every child.
</p>
