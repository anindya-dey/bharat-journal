## Where I saw it

Most urban-air-mobility plans worldwide, including India's own, focus on
short hops within a single city — airport to business district, a few
minutes in the air. A separate, less-discussed opportunity sits between
cities 100–200 km apart, too far for comfortable road travel but too
short to justify a full airport-to-airport flight — exactly the gap
eVTOL aircraft are suited to close.

## What's missing

This journal already proposes urban air mobility for the "third
dimension" within cities. What's missing is the distinct next layer:
dedicated inter-city corridors — Mumbai–Pune, Delhi–Chandigarh,
Bengaluru–Mysuru — flown point-to-point without congested city airspace or
full airport procedures at either end.

## What India already has

- **Drone Rules 2021**, which already opened Indian airspace to unmanned
  and emerging aircraft categories.
- **UDAN**, proof India can build a regional-aviation network connecting
  underserved routes quickly.
- **The urban-air-mobility proposal already in motion**, whose vertiports
  and airspace rules this corridor layer would extend, not duplicate.

## The proposal

1. **Designate two or three pilot inter-city corridors**, chosen for
   high road-traffic pairs within eVTOL range, distinct from the
   within-city vertiport network.
2. **Simplified point-to-point procedures**, avoiding full airport-style
   processing at either end, closer to an inter-city bus stop than an
   airport terminal.
3. **Share vertiport infrastructure** with the urban-air-mobility
   proposal's city-end pads, rather than building a parallel network.
4. **Start with premium and business routes**, to prove the economics
   before any commuter-scale ambition.

Ask: the Ministry of Civil Aviation and DGCA to designate pilot inter-city
eVTOL corridors as a distinct layer above the urban air-mobility network.
