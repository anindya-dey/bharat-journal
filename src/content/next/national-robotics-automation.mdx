## Where I saw it

South Korea, Germany and Japan each run manufacturing sectors with far
higher robot density per worker than India's, and critically, each
country also builds a large share of the robots themselves — an export
industry on top of a productivity tool. India's manufacturing ambitions,
from electronics to EVs, will need automation at a similar density to
compete on cost and quality.

## What's missing

India's factories are automating, but mostly through imported robotics
and automation equipment. The opportunity is to build a domestic robotics
manufacturing base alongside the adoption push, the way China combined
both for its own manufacturing rise.

## What India already has

- **A growing domestic robotics and automation startup sector**, already
  serving electronics and auto-component manufacturing.
- **PLI schemes across electronics and auto components**, which already
  create a captive domestic market for automation equipment.
- **A deep engineering-talent base** across IITs and NITs, feeding global
  robotics R&D teams already.

## The proposal

1. **A dedicated robotics-manufacturing PLI scheme**, distinct from the
   adoption incentives, rewarding Indian-built robots and automation
   systems specifically.
2. **Public robotics-and-automation testbeds** at major industrial
   clusters, so smaller manufacturers can trial automation before buying.
3. **Robotics and automation curricula** tied to ITIs and engineering
   colleges near manufacturing hubs.
4. **Publish a robot-density target**, tracked the way other manufacturing
   missions publish their own metrics.

Ask: the Ministry of Heavy Industries and MeitY to launch a dedicated
robotics-manufacturing incentive scheme alongside existing PLI programmes.
