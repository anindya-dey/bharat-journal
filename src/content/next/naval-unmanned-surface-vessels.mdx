## Where I saw it

In the Black Sea, a navy with no meaningful surface fleet of its own used
inexpensive, explosive-laden uncrewed surface vessels to damage and deter a
far larger opposing fleet. It was one of the clearest demonstrations yet
that unmanned surface vessels can shift a naval balance of power at a
fraction of the cost of a traditional warship.

## What's missing

The Indian Navy operates submarines, frigates and carriers at a scale few
navies can match, but unmanned surface vessels — for mine countermeasures,
harbour defence, chokepoint denial and long-endurance surveillance — remain
an early-stage capability rather than a fielded fleet.

## What India already has

- **DRDO and Indian shipyards' early unmanned-vessel research**, including
  autonomous surface craft demonstrators.
- **A world-class shipbuilding base**, already producing frigates and
  destroyers domestically, directly transferable to smaller autonomous
  hulls.
- **Control of key Indian Ocean chokepoints**, the exact geography where
  swarms of low-cost USVs add the most defensive value.

## The proposal

1. **A fielded USV fleet for harbour and chokepoint defence**, starting
   with India's busiest ports and the Malacca Strait approaches.
2. **Autonomous mine-countermeasure vessels**, reducing risk to crewed
   minesweepers.
3. **Long-endurance surveillance USVs** for persistent monitoring of the
   exclusive economic zone, a far cheaper alternative to continuous
   crewed-ship patrols.
4. **A domestic USV manufacturing line**, building on existing shipyards
   rather than creating a new industrial base from scratch.

Ask: DRDO and the Indian Navy to commission a fielded USV programme for
port and chokepoint defence within a fixed timeline.
