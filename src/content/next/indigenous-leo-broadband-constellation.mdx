## Where I saw it

Starlink's low-earth-orbit constellation now delivers broadband to some of
the most remote places on Earth, and OneWeb has built a competing network
with Indian industry as a partner. Both are reshaping how the last mile of
connectivity gets solved — from the ground up, to from orbit down.

## What's missing

India has welcomed foreign LEO constellations into its market and taken an
equity stake in one through Indian industry, but it does not yet have a
sovereign constellation of its own — leaving a strategic communications
layer dependent on capacity it doesn't fully control.

## What India already has

- **IN-SPACe (2020)**, which opened India's space sector to private
  players and already licenses foreign constellations to operate in India.
- **Bharti's stake in OneWeb**, proof Indian industry already has capital
  and expertise in this exact business.
- **PSLV and the growing private launch sector**, giving India the launch
  capacity a constellation would need.

## The proposal

1. **A sovereign LEO broadband constellation**, built and launched with
   Indian industry and ISRO, for BharatNet's last-mile gaps and strategic
   communications alike.
2. **Dual government-and-commercial use**, so the constellation pays for
   itself commercially while guaranteeing capacity for public and defence
   needs.
3. **Launch it on Indian rockets**, making the constellation and the
   reusable-launch programme mutually reinforcing.
4. **Open ground-equipment manufacturing to Indian industry**, so the
   value captured isn't just the satellites but the terminals too.

Ask: ISRO and IN-SPACe to commission a sovereign LEO broadband
constellation programme with Indian industry as the primary builder.
