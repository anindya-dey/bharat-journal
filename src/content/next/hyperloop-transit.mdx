## Where I saw it

Hyperloop promises city-centre to city-centre travel at airline speeds on the
ground — Mumbai to Pune in under 20 minutes. Early test tracks exist in the
US, Europe and the Gulf; the standards are still being written. Whoever helps
write them shapes the industry.

## What's missing

India's inter-city distances are ideal for ultra-high-speed transit, and its
engineering talent could drive the research. The opportunity is to be a
first mover on standards and a test corridor, not a late adopter.

## What India already has

- **Mumbai–Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail** proves India can deliver large,
  first-of-kind transit programmes.
- **IITs and Indian engineering firms** already contribute to global hyperloop
  competitions and research.
- **The Dedicated Freight Corridors** show India building dedicated
  right-of-way at scale.

## The proposal

1. **A national ultra-high-speed research programme** — a test track and
   consortium of IITs, ISRO-adjacent engineering and private industry.
2. **One pilot corridor** on a high-demand, moderate-distance route (e.g.,
   Mumbai–Pune or Delhi–Jaipur) as a technology demonstrator.
3. **Standards leadership** — contribute to international safety and
   interoperability standards early.
4. **Integrate with the rail master plan**, so ultra-high-speed is the top tier
   above HSR, RRTS and metro.

Ask: the Ministry of Railways and the Office of the Principal Scientific
Adviser to commission a feasibility study and test corridor.
