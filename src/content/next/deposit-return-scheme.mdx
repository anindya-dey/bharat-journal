## Where I saw it

Germany's Pfand system and Norway's deposit-return machines recover more
than 90% of beverage containers, by putting a small refundable deposit on
every bottle and can and a machine on every street corner to return it.
It turns litter into a transaction people want to complete.

## What's missing

India already has Extended Producer Responsibility rules and an enormous
informal recycling workforce, but there is no standard deposit-return
mechanism that makes return the easy, obvious choice at the point of
purchase. The opportunity is a small design addition, not a new industry.

## What India already has

- **EPR rules for plastics**, already in force, creating a legal basis to
  build on.
- **A vast informal recycling network** that already does the collection
  work manually.
- **[UPI](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi)**, which can make a deposit-refund near-instant rather than requiring
  physical coin return, unlike most countries' machines.

## The proposal

1. **A small refundable deposit** on bottles and cans, refunded instantly
   via UPI on return — a distinctly Indian version of the European model.
2. **Return points at kirana stores, metro stations and malls**, run by the
   existing informal recycling workforce as paid collection agents, not
   replaced by machines.
3. **Start with PET bottles and aluminium cans**, the two highest-value,
   easiest-to-sort streams.
4. **Fold it into the circular-economy framework** already proposed, as the
   collection mechanism that feeds the materials-recovery facilities.

Ask: MoEFCC and FSSAI to pilot a UPI-linked deposit-return scheme in one
metro before a national rollout.
