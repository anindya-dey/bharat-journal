## Where I saw it

Small modular reactors (SMRs) — factory-built, shippable nuclear plants —
promise firm, low-carbon power without the scale and cost of traditional
reactors. The technology is emerging globally, and the standards are still
being set.

## What's missing

India's nuclear programme is sophisticated but its capacity is small, and large
reactors are slow and capital-heavy to build. The opportunity is to develop
SMRs for industrial heat, remote grids, and coastal desalination — and to help
write the international safety standards.

## What India already has

- **India's three-stage nuclear programme** and its indigenous reactor
  technology (PHWRs) show deep capability.
- **Thorium research** gives India a long-term fuel advantage few countries
  have.
- **Global SMR momentum** — the US, UK, Canada and others are racing to
  certify designs.

## The proposal

1. **An Indian SMR programme** — develop and certify a domestic small modular
   reactor design.
2. **Target industrial and remote applications** — clean process heat for
   industry, and reliable power for remote grids and island territories.
3. **Pair SMRs with desalination** — coastal nuclear-powered water plants to
   drought-proof cities.
4. **Standards leadership** — contribute to international SMR safety and
   licensing frameworks.

Ask: the Department of Atomic Energy to commission an SMR feasibility and
design programme.
