## Where I saw it

The US elevated its space operations into a full, independent service arm
— the Space Force — recognising that satellites for navigation,
communication and early warning have become contested military
infrastructure, not just support systems. Several other major powers are
following with their own dedicated space-military structures.

## What's missing

India has already demonstrated serious counter-space capability — the 2019
anti-satellite test made it one of a handful of nations to do so — and
runs a tri-service Defence Space Agency. The opportunity is to grow that
agency's mandate, budget and independence to match the scale of the threat
it already proved it could counter.

## What India already has

- **The Defence Space Agency**, a tri-service body already coordinating
  India's military space operations.
- **Mission Shakti (2019)**, India's demonstrated anti-satellite
  capability.
- **ISRO's launch and satellite infrastructure**, a deep civilian base the
  military space mission already draws on.

## The proposal

1. **Elevate the Defence Space Agency** toward a fully independent command,
   with its own budget line and career track, as satellite dependency
   grows across every service.
2. **Give it operational command over the space-situational-awareness
   network and counter-space interceptor fleet already proposed in this
   journal**, so tracking and response sit under one chain of command
   rather than being built as unconnected efforts.
3. **Counter-space doctrine published responsibly**, distinguishing
   defensive and deterrent capability from offensive escalation, to guide
   how and when capability like Mission Shakti is used.
4. **Closer fusion with ISRO and private space companies**, so military
   space capability grows alongside, not separate from, India's civilian
   space economy.

Ask: the Ministry of Defence to publish a roadmap elevating the Defence
Space Agency into a fully resourced tri-service command.
