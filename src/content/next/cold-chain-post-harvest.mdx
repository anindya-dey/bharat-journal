## Where I saw it

The Netherlands and Israel, operating in far harsher land and water
constraints than India, lose only a small fraction of what they grow
between the farm and the final buyer, because cold storage, grading and
transport are built as continuous infrastructure, not patched together
after the fact. India's farmers often lose the value of their harvest well
before it even gets to a middleman.

## What's missing

India is one of the world's largest producers of fruit and vegetables, but
a large share is lost or degraded in quality before reaching a buyer, for
want of cold storage near the farm gate, refrigerated transport, and
grading infrastructure. The loss falls hardest on the smallholder, who has
no way to wait for a better price.

## What India already has

- **Mega Food Parks and the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana**, already
  funding food-processing and storage infrastructure.
- **A fast-growing organised cold-chain logistics industry**, serving
  pharma and e-commerce, that could extend into agriculture.
- **FPOs (Farmer Producer Organisations)**, an aggregation structure that
  makes shared cold-storage investment viable for smallholders.

## The proposal

1. **Farm-gate cold storage for the top loss-prone crops**, funded through
   FPOs so smallholders can access shared infrastructure.
2. **A refrigerated-transport network** connecting farm clusters to
   wholesale markets and ports, not just isolated storage depots.
3. **Grading and packaging standards** at the point of harvest, so quality
   loss is caught early, not after transport.
4. **Publish a national post-harvest-loss number annually**, the way other
   missions publish a tracked metric, so the mission is measured against
   it.

Ask: the Ministry of Food Processing Industries to publish a national
post-harvest-loss reduction target with FPO-anchored cold-chain funding.
