## Where I saw it

Space-based solar power — a satellite that collects sunlight continuously,
unfiltered by atmosphere or night, and beams the energy to Earth as
microwaves — is being researched by Japan, the US and China, among others,
but no one has demonstrated it at meaningful scale yet. It remains a
genuinely open frontier, not a race already being won by someone else.

## What's missing

India has two of the exact ingredients this technology needs — a mature
solar-power industry and a capable space programme — but has not yet
combined them into a dedicated space-solar research effort the way it has
combined, for example, solar and hydrogen.

## What India already has

- **150+ GW of installed solar and wind capacity**, and some of the
  world's lowest solar tariffs, proof of deep solar-engineering expertise.
- **ISRO's satellite-power and antenna research**, directly relevant to
  the orbital collection and beaming hardware this requires.
- **DST and ISRO's joint research programmes**, an existing structure to
  house a new demonstrator project.

## The proposal

1. **Fund a small-scale orbital solar-collection demonstrator**, testing
   power beaming to a ground receiver before any commitment to a
   full-scale system.
2. **Pair India's solar-panel manufacturing base** with the satellite
   programme, rather than treating them as separate industries.
3. **Research the ground-receiver and grid-integration side** in
   parallel, since beaming power is only useful if the grid can absorb it.
4. **Publish results openly**, building India's credibility as a serious
   early entrant in a technology still being defined globally.

Ask: ISRO and DST to jointly fund a small-scale space-based solar power
demonstrator mission.
