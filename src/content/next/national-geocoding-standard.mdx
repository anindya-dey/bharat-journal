## Where I saw it

Countries with unified national geocoding — a single precise code for every
location, down to a few metres — make deliveries, emergency response and
public records dramatically more reliable than text addresses ever can be,
especially where street naming is inconsistent.

## What's missing

India's addresses are often descriptive rather than precise — a landmark, a
lane, a floor — which slows deliveries, logistics and emergency response.
India Post's Digipin is a real step toward a unified digital address grid;
the opportunity is to make it the backbone every service actually uses, not
a parallel system alongside postal codes.

## What India already has

- **Digipin**, India Post's digital geocoding grid, already assigns a
  precise code to every location in the country.
- **[Aadhaar](https://uidai.gov.in/) and e-KYC** already link identity to address at national scale.
- **India's logistics and e-commerce sector**, already building private
  geocoding layers that a public standard could unify.

## The proposal

1. **Mandate Digipin in government forms and services** — Aadhaar updates,
   voter rolls, ration cards and welfare delivery — so the grid becomes the
   default address.
2. **Open APIs for private use**, so delivery, ride-hailing and emergency
   apps adopt it instead of building their own geocoding.
3. **Link Digipin to emergency response (112)**, so a caller's location is
   precise by default, not dependent on a description.
4. **Rural rollout first**, where descriptive addresses fail most often and
   the gain is largest.

Ask: the Department of Posts and MeitY to mandate Digipin across government
digital services within a fixed timeline.
