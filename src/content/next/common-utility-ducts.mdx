## Where I saw it

In Japan and Singapore, power cables, water pipes, gas lines and fibre all
run through shared underground utility ducts, accessible through manholes
without ever breaking the road surface. A new fibre provider doesn't mean a
new trench — it means access to space already built for exactly that.

## What's missing

Indian cities re-dig the same roads repeatedly as different utilities lay
cable, pipe and fibre independently, with no shared duct to use instead.
The cost shows up as traffic disruption, broken pavements, and utilities
that damage each other because no one maps what's already buried.

## What India already has

- **[Smart Cities Mission](https://smartcities.gov.in/)** has already funded underground utility corridors in
  some redevelopment areas.
- **GIS-based utility mapping** pilots exist in a few cities, a precondition
  for shared ducts.
- **[BharatNet](https://www.bbnl.nic.in/)**, which needs exactly this kind of shared conduit as it reaches
  the country's last villages.

## The proposal

1. **Mandatory shared utility ducts** for all new and major-redevelopment
   roads, sized for power, water, gas and fibre together.
2. **A national underground-utility map**, so any agency digging knows
   what's already there before it breaks ground.
3. **Retrofit priority corridors** — the most frequently re-dug roads in the
   largest cities — as the first targets.
4. **Cost-sharing rules** across utilities, so no single agency bears the
   full cost of ducts everyone will use.

Ask: the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs and the Smart Cities Mission to
mandate shared utility ducts in all new road construction standards.
