## Where I saw it

The US NEXRAD network and Japan's radar mesh give forecasters a live, dense
picture of storms building in real time, down to the next hour over a
specific neighbourhood — "nowcasting" rather than the next day's forecast.
India's monsoon bursts and flash floods move just as fast, and the cost of
missing them, in a flood-prone megacity, is measured in lives.

## What's missing

India's weather-radar network has expanded but is far less dense than
comparable monsoon-exposed countries, and hyperlocal, hour-ahead forecasts
for specific neighbourhoods are not yet a standard public service during
extreme rainfall events.

## What India already has

- **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** already runs a national radar
  and forecasting network, expanded significantly in the last decade.
- **[Mausam](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/) and the Common Alerting Protocol** already deliver warnings to
  phones during cyclones.
- A growing base of **AI-based nowcasting research** at Indian institutes.

## The proposal

1. **Fill the radar gaps**, with priority for the most flood-prone metros
   and the monsoon-exposed coastline.
2. **Hyperlocal, hour-ahead nowcasting**, pushed to phones by locality, not
   just district-level forecasts.
3. **Link nowcasting to city drainage and traffic systems**, so warnings
   trigger pump operations and route diversions automatically.
4. **Open the radar data** for researchers and private forecasting apps to
   build on, the way the US does with NEXRAD.

Ask: the India Meteorological Department and the Ministry of Earth Sciences
to publish a radar-density roadmap prioritising India's most flood-exposed
cities.
