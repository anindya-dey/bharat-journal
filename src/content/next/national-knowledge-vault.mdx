## Where I saw it

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, buried deep inside a Norwegian mountain,
exists for one reason: no single library, lab, gene bank or country
should ever be the only copy of something irreplaceable. That logic isn't
specific to seeds — it applies to manuscripts, genomic data, cultural
artefacts, critical source code and anything else a country cannot afford
to lose to a single fire, flood, conflict or cyberattack.

## What's missing

India runs several valuable but separate preservation efforts — a
manuscripts mission, a plant gene bank, digital archives — each
concentrated substantially at one or two primary sites. None of them is
designed, by itself, to survive the worst-case loss of its one home. What's
missing isn't preservation; it's a shared doctrine of redundancy applied
across all of them at once, the way Svalbard applies it to seeds alone.

## What India already has

- **The National Mission for Manuscripts**, already digitising millions
  of ancient Indian texts.
- **The National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources' gene bank**, already
  preserving a vast collection of seed and plant accessions, concentrated
  substantially at one primary facility.
- **India's Himalayan and peninsular geology**, offering genuinely secure
  mountain and underground sites, the same kind of terrain Norway used
  for its seed vault.
- **CDAC and India's data-centre industry**, already capable of building
  hardened, long-duration digital storage.

## The proposal

**One vault network, not one vault.** The container matters less than the
rule inside it: nothing irreplaceable should have only one copy, anywhere.

1. **A single national vault doctrine covering every type of
   irreplaceable material** — manuscripts and oral-tradition recordings,
   seed and plant genetics, livestock semen and embryo lines, scientific
   and genomic datasets, cultural artefacts and museum-grade replicas, and
   the source code, keys and configuration behind India's critical digital
   public infrastructure.
2. **At least three geographically separated sites for every category** —
   Himalayan, peninsular and, where feasible, a seabed-adjacent coastal
   site — so no single regional disaster threatens any one type of
   material completely.
3. **Full duplication, not partial backup** — the National Bureau of
   Plant Genetic Resources' gene bank, the manuscripts mission's archive,
   and every other covered collection fully cloned across the vault
   network's sites, not sampled or summarised.
4. **Both physical and digital preservation in parallel**, pairing
   original material and specimens with redundant, offline digital
   copies, so a loss of the physical object doesn't mean a loss of the
   record.
5. **Share engineering with the underground-research-cities and
   continuity-of-government proposals already in this journal**, rather
   than building three separate sets of deep-construction expertise for
   three related purposes.
6. **International-standard preservation protocols** throughout, so every
   category meets the same durability bar Svalbard sets for seeds, not a
   symbolic lesser version for everything else.

Ask: the Ministry of Culture, DST and ICAR to jointly adopt one national
vault doctrine and fund the first three duplicated sites, starting with
the manuscripts and plant-genetics collections already being digitised
and catalogued.
