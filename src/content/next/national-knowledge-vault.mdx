## Where I saw it

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, buried deep inside a Norwegian mountain,
exists for one reason: no single library, lab or country should be the
only copy of something irreplaceable. The same logic that protects crop
diversity applies just as well to knowledge itself — manuscripts,
scientific data, and the digital record of a civilisation, all vulnerable
to fire, conflict, cyberattack or simple decay if only one copy exists.

## What's missing

India holds an immense and still-growing body of knowledge — ancient
manuscripts and oral traditions, modern scientific and genomic data, and
the records of its own digital-public-infrastructure systems — but no
dedicated, geographically distributed vault system designed to survive a
worst-case loss of any single site.

## What India already has

- **The National Mission for Manuscripts**, already digitising millions
  of ancient Indian texts.
- **India's Himalayan and peninsular geology**, offering genuinely secure
  mountain and underground sites, the same kind of terrain Norway used
  for its seed vault.
- **CDAC and India's data-centre industry**, already capable of building
  hardened, long-duration digital storage.

## The proposal

1. **At least three geographically separated vault sites** — Himalayan,
   peninsular and, where feasible, a seabed-adjacent coastal site — so no
   single regional disaster threatens the whole archive.
2. **Both physical and digital preservation**, pairing original
   manuscripts and specimens with redundant, offline digital copies.
3. **Open scope beyond heritage** — genomic data, scientific archives and
   critical digital-infrastructure backups belong in the same system.
4. **International-standard preservation protocols**, so the vaults meet
   the same durability bar as Svalbard's, not a symbolic lesser version.

Ask: the Ministry of Culture and DST to jointly fund a multi-site national
knowledge vault, physical and digital, starting with the manuscripts
already being digitised.
