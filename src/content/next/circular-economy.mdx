## Where I saw it

A circular economy — where materials are recovered, reused and remanufactured
rather than buried — is both an environmental and an industrial strategy.
Europe and Japan treat waste as a resource; India has the volume and the
informal recycling workforce to lead.

## What's missing

India already has one of the world's highest informal recycling rates, but it
is fragmented, unsafe and undervalued. The opportunity is to formalise and
industrialise it: turn the country's waste streams into recovered materials
and energy, and reduce dependence on imported scrap and raw materials.

## What India already has

- **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** rules already cover plastics and
  e-waste.
- **Waste-to-energy and biogas plants** operate in several cities.
- India's **informal recyclers** recover a large share of materials with
  minimal infrastructure — a base to build on, not replace.

## The proposal

1. **Integrated materials-recovery facilities** in every large city — sorting,
   recycling and remanufacturing, co-located with waste-to-energy and biogas.
2. **Formalise and protect the informal workforce** — safety, fair prices and
   training for the recyclers already doing the work.
3. **A recovered-materials market** — standards, procurement preferences, and
   incentives for products made from recycled content.
4. **Urban mining for e-waste** — recover the metals in India's discarded
   electronics domestically instead of exporting the value.

Ask: MoEFCC and MoHUA to publish a national circular-economy framework with
city-level recovery targets.
