## Where I saw it

Joby, Archer and EHang are flight-testing electric air taxis; Dubai,
Singapore and São Paulo are preparing launch corridors for short hops
within a city — airport to business district, a few minutes in the air.
A separate, less-discussed opportunity sits between cities 100–200 km
apart, too far for comfortable road travel but too short to justify a
full airport-to-airport flight — Mumbai–Pune in the air, not just on the
ground. Both tiers use the same aircraft and much of the same
infrastructure; they just need two different sets of rules.

## What's missing

India has the demand case almost no one else has: enormous metros,
brutal congestion, and a booming aviation market, for both the within-
city hop and the inter-city gap. What's missing is the enabling layer for
each — airspace rules for low-altitude city flight, certified vertiports,
a domestic eVTOL industry, and, for the longer tier, simplified
point-to-point procedures that don't require full airport processing at
either end.

## What India already has

- **Drone Rules 2021**, which already opened Indian airspace to unmanned
  and emerging aircraft categories, creating a domestic drone industry
  almost overnight.
- **[UDAN](https://www.civilaviation.gov.in/)**, proof India can build a regional-aviation network connecting
  underserved routes quickly — the model the inter-city tier would
  extend into the air.
- **Indian startups and HAL**, already exploring eVTOL designs.

## The proposal

**Tier 1 — Within the city.** A low-altitude airspace framework —
digital corridors, traffic management, and certification standards for
eVTOL aircraft — with vertiports at transit hubs: rooftop and
station-adjacent pads at metro termini, airports and business districts,
planned into new cities. Start with emergency and premium use — medical
organ transfer, disaster response, and airport transfers — before
scaling to daily commuting.

**Tier 2 — Between nearby cities.** Once the within-city tier is
running, designate two or three pilot inter-city corridors — Mumbai–Pune,
Delhi–Chandigarh, Bengaluru–Mysuru — chosen for high road-traffic pairs
within eVTOL range, flown point-to-point with simplified procedures
closer to an inter-city bus stop than an airport terminal. Share
vertiport infrastructure with Tier 1's city-end pads rather than building
a parallel network, and start with premium and business routes to prove
the economics first.

**Across both tiers:** an eVTOL manufacturing incentive, positioning
India as a builder, not just a buyer, of the next aviation segment.

Ask: the Ministry of Civil Aviation and DGCA to publish one national
air-taxi roadmap covering both the within-city vertiport network and
inter-city pilot corridors, rather than treating them as separate plans.
