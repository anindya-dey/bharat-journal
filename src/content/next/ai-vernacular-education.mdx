## Where I saw it

AI tutors have already shown they can lift learning in one-to-one settings —
the promise of a private tutor for every child, once imaginable only for the
wealthy. India, with its translation stack and digital rails, is uniquely
placed to deliver that promise at population scale.

## What's missing

India's greatest educational asset is its linguistic diversity — and its
greatest delivery challenge is the same. A child in a Hindi, Tamil, Bangla or
Bodo-medium home needs learning in the language they think in, from a system
that adapts to their pace. The opportunity is to make that the default, not a
privilege.

## What India already has

- **[DIKSHA](https://diksha.gov.in/)** already hosts curriculum-linked digital content at national
  scale.
- **[Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/)** provides open, high-quality translation and speech across
  Indian languages.
- **The National Digital University** (2022) and NDEAR give the delivery
  architecture.
- India's own **language models** are maturing rapidly.

## The proposal

1. **A mother-tongue AI tutor** for every child — curriculum-aligned, adaptive,
   and voice-first, so it works for early readers and low-literacy homes.
2. **A teacher co-pilot** that drafts lessons, generates practice sets, and
   flags where a class is struggling — so teachers teach, not administer.
3. **Offline-first design** — the tutor works without constant connectivity,
   syncing when a signal is available.
4. **Open and auditable** — built on public models and standards, so states
   and boards can adapt it and researchers can check its outputs.

Ask: the Ministry of Education, with DIKSHA and Bhashini, to pilot an
AI tutor in three languages across a few districts before scaling.
