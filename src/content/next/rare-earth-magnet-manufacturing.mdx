## Where I saw it

A single country refines the overwhelming majority of the world's rare
earths and manufactures most of the permanent magnets made from them —
the components inside EV motors, wind-turbine generators and precision-
guided weapons. Export restrictions from that one source have already
rattled automakers and defence planners elsewhere, a vulnerability no
country wants to carry indefinitely.

## What's missing

India holds meaningful rare-earth reserves but has very limited domestic
refining and almost no magnet-manufacturing capacity, so the minerals in
the ground don't translate into the finished magnets India's EV, wind and
defence industries need. This sits squarely inside the broader
strategic-resource-independence doctrine already proposed, but is urgent
and specific enough to merit its own dedicated push.

## What India already has

- **IREL (India Rare Earths Limited)**, a public-sector company already
  mining and partially processing rare-earth minerals.
- **Known rare-earth-bearing mineral sands** along several coastal
  states, an under-exploited domestic resource base.
- **A fast-growing EV and wind-turbine manufacturing base**, the exact
  domestic customer this supply chain would serve.

## The proposal

1. **Fund rare-earth refining capacity at scale**, not just mining, so
   India processes its own reserves rather than exporting raw material
   and re-importing refined metal.
2. **A domestic magnet-manufacturing industry**, incentivised the way
   battery-cell manufacturing already is under the PLI scheme.
3. **Diversify import partnerships** for any shortfall, following the
   minimum-two-source doctrine already proposed for strategic inputs.
4. **Prioritise defence and EV-motor-grade magnets first**, the two
   applications with the clearest strategic urgency.

Ask: the Ministry of Mines to fund rare-earth refining and magnet-
manufacturing capacity as a dedicated strategic-minerals mission.
