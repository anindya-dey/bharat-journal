## The Spark

Quantum technology is the next computing paradigm — able to solve problems
classical computers cannot, and to make communication provably secure. The
countries that build capability now will set the standards for the next fifty
years. India moved early.

## The Gap

India has world-class physics talent and a dedicated mission, but the
frontier moves fast. The opportunity is to concentrate resources into a few
globally competitive hubs and a real quantum communication network, rather
than spreading them thin.

## The Precedent

- **National Quantum Mission** (2023, ~₹6,000 crore) across computing,
  communication, sensing and materials.
- **Quantum labs** at IISc, IITs and DRDO, and a growing quantum startup base.
- India's strength in **cryptography and secure systems**, a natural adjacent
  capability.

## The Proposal

1. **Four to six national quantum hubs**, each world-class in one area —
   quantum computing, communication, sensing, or materials.
2. **A secure quantum communication network** (QKD links) between key
   government, financial and research sites, as a template for a national
   quantum-safe backbone.
3. **Quantum-safe encryption migration** for India's digital public
   infrastructure, so UPI, Aadhaar and government systems are ready before
   quantum computers can break today's cryptography.
4. **A quantum talent pipeline** — PhDs, fellowships and startup funding tied
   to the hubs.

Ask: expand the National Quantum Mission with a clear target for a working
quantum communication link and quantum-safe standards for public systems.
