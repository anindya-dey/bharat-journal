## Where I saw it

Japan's 2010 rare-earth supply shock, and the more recent scramble among
Western economies to diversify battery-mineral and semiconductor supply
chains away from single dominant suppliers, taught the same lesson twice:
a country that depends on one source for a scarce, critical input doesn't
control its own industrial future — it has outsourced a veto on it.

## What's missing

India depends heavily on single or near-single sources for several
strategic inputs at once — rare-earth magnets, battery-grade lithium and
cobalt, active pharmaceutical ingredients, and some fertiliser feedstocks
among them. Each has its own ministry and its own partial fix underway,
but there is no single standing doctrine that treats "never depend on one
country for a scarce input" as a design rule applied everywhere at once.

## What India already has

- **PLI schemes for advanced-chemistry cells, bulk drugs and
  semiconductors**, each independently reducing one dependency.
- **Critical-minerals partnerships** being negotiated with resource-rich
  countries across several continents.
- **Strategic petroleum reserves**, proof India already applies this
  doctrine to at least one commodity — oil.

## The proposal

1. **A published list of strategic-dependency inputs**, reviewed annually,
   the way the oil-reserve doctrine already treats one commodity.
2. **A minimum-two-source rule** for each listed input — domestic
   production plus at least one diversified import partner, never a
   single country.
3. **Strategic reserves for the top-tier inputs**, beyond oil, sized to
   cover a multi-month supply disruption.
4. **One coordinating body** across NITI Aayog, Mines, Commerce and
   External Affairs, so dependency strategy isn't split across ministries
   that don't talk to each other.

Ask: NITI Aayog to publish a national strategic-dependency list and a
minimum-two-source doctrine applied across every ministry that imports a
critical input.
