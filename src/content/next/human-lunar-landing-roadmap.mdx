## Where I saw it

NASA's Artemis programme and China's crewed lunar ambitions both work
backward from a dated landing target to a funded sequence of
intermediate missions — a space station, a lander, a return vehicle — each
de-risking the next. A goal without that sequence tends to slip
indefinitely; a goal with it tends to happen.

## What's missing

India has stated an ambition to land an astronaut on the Moon and has
Gaganyaan and the Bharatiya Antariksh Station as building blocks already
underway. What's missing is the published, funded intermediate sequence —
uncrewed lunar lander tests, a crewed lunar orbit mission, then a landing —
that turns the ambition into a roadmap.

## What India already has

- **Gaganyaan**, India's human-spaceflight programme, already in
  development.
- **Chandrayaan-3**, which proved a soft, precision lunar landing near the
  south pole — the robotic precursor to a crewed one.
- **The Bharatiya Antariksh Station**, planned for the mid-2030s, a
  natural staging point for lunar missions.

## The proposal

1. **Publish the intermediate mission sequence** — uncrewed lunar lander
   test, crewed lunar flyby, then landing — each with its own funded
   milestone.
2. **Use the Bharatiya Antariksh Station as a staging and testing point**
   for lunar hardware before committing to the landing mission itself.
3. **Build the heavy-lift launch capability** a crewed lunar mission needs,
   as its own tracked programme.
4. **Invite international partnership selectively**, on tasks that don't
   compromise India's own capability-building along the way.

Ask: ISRO to publish a funded, dated intermediate-mission roadmap from
Gaganyaan to a crewed lunar landing.
