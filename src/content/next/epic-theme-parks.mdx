## Where I saw it

Universal Studios Japan. The Warner Bros. Harry Potter studio tour in
London. People cross oceans and spend days inside story-worlds. Korea's K-culture theme zones, China's Universal Beijing
Resort — the world's biggest tourism economies have all discovered that
**a story, made physical, is an export-grade attraction**.

## What's missing

India has huge domestic studio and park assets to build on — Ramoji Film
City (Hyderabad), India's largest studio complex, and Adlabs Imagica's
early theme-park experiments — but no globally-branded story-immersion
park of the scale of Universal or Disney. Ayodhya has been developing
Ramayana-themed attractions; this idea stands on its own regardless of
that project's timeline.

Meanwhile, India owns the raw material no one else has: **two national
epics** with battle scenes, flying machines, exile journeys, and
moral dilemmas — material Disney would pay billions to option, and
India holds the rights in its collective consciousness.

## What India already has

- **Universal Beijing Resort** — opened 2021, ~$6.5B invested; a
  case study in studio + state partnership.
- **The Lord of the Rings economy** — New Zealand's ~NZ$600M/yr
  tourism pull from one film trilogy's landscapes.
- **Japan's Studio Ghibli park** — story-first, no rides: proof
  that immersion ≠ roller coasters.

## The proposal

Two parallel tracks:

1. **"Epic India" national parks** — a public-private programme for
   2-4 world-class story-parks: one Ramayan (Ayodhya or a Uttar
   Pradesh site), one Mahabharat (Kurukshetra — already owns the
   land and the story), with global creative/hardware partners
   (Universal/Disney/Chimelong-grade operators on the RFP list),
   run as long-term concessions, not vanity projects.
2. **Invite the global studios to Bharat** — a dedicated "Studio
   India" policy: land + tax incentives + story-world licensing
   partnerships for a Universal/Disney/Legoland park in the NCR or
   Mumbai region, paired with a "produce Indian IP" obligation
   (the Harry Potter playbook: build the park, sell the story,
   export the merchandise).

The economics write themselves: a Universal-grade park in India's
domestic-tourism catchment (1.4B people, a middle class larger than
the US population) has no peer in terms of demand-side fundamentals.

Ask: Ministry of Tourism + Invest India + the states (land bank) —
begin with a feasibility RFP, not land acquisition.
