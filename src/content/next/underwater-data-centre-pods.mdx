## Where I saw it

A well-known undersea data-centre pilot submerged sealed server pods off
the Scottish coast, using the surrounding seawater to cool the equipment
passively instead of power-hungry air conditioning, and found the sealed,
oxygen-free environment also cut hardware failure rates. The pilot ran
for years and was retrieved successfully — but no country has yet turned
the concept into standing commercial or public infrastructure.

## What's missing

India's data-centre-grid proposal already in this journal calls for
hubs near every state capital, renewably powered. Coastal cities carry an
additional, unused option: underwater pods that cool themselves for free
using the sea, cutting one of a data centre's largest ongoing costs,
energy for cooling, without adding to land use in dense coastal metros.

## What India already has

- **The national data-centre-grid proposal already in this journal**,
  which this would extend specifically for coastal hubs.
- **India's long coastline and major coastal metros** — Mumbai, Chennai,
  Visakhapatnam — each already a data-centre hub under conventional
  cooling.
- **A fast-growing domestic data-centre industry**, already investing
  heavily in new capacity and open to cost-saving cooling innovation.

## The proposal

1. **A pilot underwater data-centre pod** at one coastal hub, built and
   operated with an Indian data-centre major, to validate the cooling and
   maintenance model in Indian waters.
2. **Target AI training workloads first**, where power density and
   cooling costs are highest and the savings are most significant.
3. **Environmental review of local marine impact**, since any heat
   exchange with seawater needs ecological safeguards.
4. **Fold successful pilots into the national data-centre-grid's coastal
   nodes**, rather than running it as a standalone experiment.

Ask: MeitY and the National AI Mission to fund a pilot underwater data-
centre pod with an Indian data-centre operator at one coastal hub.
