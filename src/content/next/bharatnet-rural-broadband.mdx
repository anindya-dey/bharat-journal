## Where I saw it

India's mobile revolution reached almost everyone, and 70% of the population is
now online. But access is not the same as *use* — the rural-urban digital gap
persists in speed, reliability and the services built on top.

## What's missing

The fibre backbone exists but the last mile is unfinished, and rural users
often get slower, costlier connections. The opportunity is to complete the
network and — just as important — build services that make connectivity
*useful* in a village: telemedicine, e-learning, digital government, and
market access for local producers.

## What India already has

- **BharatNet** — the national optical-fibre programme to connect gram
  panchayats.
- **The mobile data revolution** — among the cheapest data in the world.
- **Digital India and Common Service Centres** — a delivery network already
  reaching villages.

## The ask

1. **Finish the last-mile fibre** — every village and public institution
   connected, with reliable backhaul.
2. **Affordable rural connectivity** — subsidised or bundled access for
   schools, health centres and panchayats.
3. **Services that matter** — telemedicine, e-learning, digital banking and
   market access built for rural users, in local languages.
4. **Community Wi-Fi hubs** — public access points so a connection is shared,
   not rationed per household.
