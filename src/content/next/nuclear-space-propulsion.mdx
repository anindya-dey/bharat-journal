## Where I saw it

Chemical rockets are reaching the limits of what they can do for
deep-space missions — getting to Mars or beyond faster, with more cargo,
needs a fundamentally different engine. NASA and DARPA's DRACO programme,
developing a nuclear thermal propulsion spacecraft, is essentially the
only active effort in the world working on this at a serious level.

## What's missing

India has deep nuclear engineering expertise through its atomic-energy
programme and a capable space agency, but the two have never been
combined into a nuclear-space-propulsion research effort — a genuinely
open field where India's existing strengths overlap almost no one else's.

## What India already has

- **India's three-stage nuclear programme and indigenous reactor
  technology**, proof of deep nuclear-engineering capability.
- **ISRO's deep-space mission experience**, from Mars and the Sun to a
  planned Venus mission.
- **Thorium research**, giving India nuclear expertise few other
  spacefaring nations have invested in as heavily.

## The proposal

1. **A joint DAE-ISRO nuclear-propulsion research programme**, starting
   with small-scale nuclear thermal engine research rather than a flight
   mission.
2. **Target deep-space cargo missions first**, where propulsion
   efficiency matters most and the risk tolerance for new technology is
   higher than for crewed missions.
3. **Build the regulatory and safety framework early**, in parallel with
   the engineering, given how sensitive nuclear-in-space programmes are
   treated internationally.
4. **Publish research openly where possible**, to build credibility as a
   genuine research partner rather than a closed military programme.

Ask: the Department of Atomic Energy and ISRO to jointly fund early-stage
nuclear space-propulsion research.
