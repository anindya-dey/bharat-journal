## Where I saw it

Compute is the electricity of this century. Singapore and Ireland built
data-centre parks that anchor whole digital economies; the AI era has made
hyperscale capacity a matter of national capability, not just commerce. In
India, data-centre capacity is already booming — but concentrated.

## What's missing

India's data centres cluster in a handful of metros (Mumbai, Chennai,
Hyderabad). That leaves the rest of the country dependent on distant
capacity, exposes the network to single-point risks, and pushes the AI
workloads of the future into a few grids. The opportunity is a *distributed*
grid: sovereign compute close to every capital, spread across the country's
power and fibre map.

## What India already has

- **Indian industry is ready**: Reliance/Jio, Adani, Tata, Bharti and CtrlS are
  all investing heavily in data centres and green power.
- **The National AI Mission** has committed to public AI compute capacity.
- **India's renewable build-out** (150+ GW and rising) gives these hubs clean
  power at the source.

## The proposal

1. **One sovereign data-centre hub per state capital** (or in its satellite
   belt), developed with Indian majors on long-term concessions.
2. **Power them renewably** — co-locate with solar/wind parks and connect to
   the national grid, so compute growth doesn't raise emissions.
3. **Tier the grid** — a few hyperscale national nodes plus regional hubs, so
   AI training, disaster recovery, and public-service hosting all have homes.
4. **Sovereignty by design** — data residency, national-security-grade
   isolation for sensitive workloads, and public compute quotas for
   startups, researchers and government.

Ask: MeitY and the National AI Mission, with state IT departments, to publish
a national compute-grid blueprint and open the first co-invested hubs.
