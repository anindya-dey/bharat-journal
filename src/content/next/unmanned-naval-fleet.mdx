## Where I saw it

In the Black Sea, a navy with no meaningful surface fleet of its own used
inexpensive, explosive-laden uncrewed surface vessels to damage and deter
a far larger opposing fleet — one of the clearest demonstrations yet that
unmanned surface vessels can shift a naval balance of power at a fraction
of the cost of a traditional warship. Below the surface, extra-large
unmanned underwater vehicle programmes in the US and China aim to patrol,
lay sensors, and even strike, for months at a time, without risking a
crewed submarine or its sailors. Both are the same idea applied to a
different medium: autonomy changes the economics of naval presence.

## What's missing

The Indian Navy operates submarines, frigates and carriers at a scale few
navies can match, but unmanned vessels — surface craft for mine
countermeasures, harbour defence and chokepoint denial, and underwater
vehicles for anti-submarine warfare and long-endurance surveillance —
remain early-stage research rather than a fielded fleet in either medium.

## What India already has

- **DRDO and Indian shipyards' early unmanned-vessel research**, including
  autonomous surface craft demonstrators.
- **A world-class shipbuilding base**, already producing frigates,
  destroyers and submarines domestically, directly transferable to
  smaller autonomous hulls in both mediums.
- **The Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan (Matsya-6000)**, proving India
  can design and operate crewed deep-sea submersibles — underwater
  engineering talent a military UUV programme could share rather than
  duplicate.
- **Control of key Indian Ocean chokepoints**, the exact geography where
  swarms of low-cost unmanned vessels, surface or underwater, add the
  most defensive value.

## The proposal

1. **A fielded USV fleet for harbour and chokepoint defence**, starting
   with India's busiest ports and the Malacca Strait approaches, including
   autonomous mine-countermeasure vessels to reduce risk to crewed
   minesweepers.
2. **A military-grade UUV programme for anti-submarine warfare and
   mine-hunting**, distinct from the scientific Samudrayaan effort, but
   sharing underwater-engineering talent with the Deep Ocean Mission
   rather than building it twice.
3. **Long-endurance variants in both mediums** for persistent surveillance
   of the exclusive economic zone and the Indian Ocean's chokepoints, a
   far cheaper alternative to continuous crewed-ship patrols.
4. **A domestic unmanned-vessel manufacturing line**, building on existing
   shipyards for both surface and underwater hulls, rather than creating
   two separate industrial bases.

Ask: DRDO and the Indian Navy to commission a fielded unmanned naval
fleet programme covering both surface and underwater vehicles, sharing
engineering talent with the Deep Ocean Mission where the two overlap.
