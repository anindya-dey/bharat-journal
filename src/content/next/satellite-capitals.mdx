## Where I saw it

The world's most liveable capitals are not single cities — they are
constellations. Brasília was purpose-built inland; Putrajaya gave Malaysia an
administrative counterweight; Indonesia is building Nusantara from scratch;
Delhi's own NCR grew Gurgaon, Noida and Ghaziabad into de-facto satellites.
The lesson is that a capital works best as a *network*, not a single
pressure point.

## What's missing

India's capitals — national and state alike — concentrate the jobs,
universities, hospitals, airports and government offices that everyone must
reach, in one geographic spot. The opportunity is to plan the counterweights
deliberately instead of letting them sprawl: two or three satellite cities
per capital region, each a complete place to live and work, tied to the core
by fast transit.

## What India already has

- **[Namo Bharat](https://ncrtc.in/) RRTS** (Delhi–Meerut, operational 2023) shows India can build
  regional rapid transit at scale.
- **The Mumbai–Ahmedabad bullet train**, under construction, proves
  high-speed inter-city links are within reach.
- **Chandigarh and Gandhinagar** are reminders that India already knows how to
  build planned capitals.

## The proposal

1. **Two to three satellites per capital region**, each planned as a
   self-sufficient district: housing, schools, hospitals, an employment base,
   and a green core — not dormitory towns.
2. **Connect them with high-speed rail or RRTS**, so the satellite-to-core
   commute is 20–30 minutes, and the satellites are linked to each other.
3. **Move some government and institutional load out** — a high court bench,
   a university cluster, a medical hub, a stadium — so the satellites have
   reasons to exist from day one.
4. **Build in resilience** — independent water, power, airport and data
   capacity, so each satellite can serve as a contingency hub if the capital
   is ever disrupted.

Ask: NITI Aayog and the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs to commission a
national "counterweight cities" framework, piloted around two or three state
capitals first.
