## Where I saw it

Precision agriculture in the US and Israel uses GPS-guided equipment,
soil-moisture sensors and satellite imagery to apply exactly the water,
fertiliser and seed a specific patch of land needs, square metre by
square metre, instead of a uniform rate across a whole field. It raises
yield and cuts input waste at the same time — a rare both-sides win.

## What's missing

India already collects enormous amounts of relevant data — the Soil
Health Card programme has tested soil nutrients at huge scale — but most
of that data isn't yet connected to variable-rate equipment or farmer
decisions in real time. The data exists; the last-mile action on it
mostly doesn't.

## What India already has

- **The Soil Health Card scheme**, already testing and reporting soil
  nutrient data for hundreds of millions of farm holdings.
- **PM Kisan Drones and satellite-imagery programmes**, already
  generating the remote-sensing layer precision agriculture needs.
- **A fast-growing agri-tech sector**, already building sensor and
  advisory apps for Indian farmers.

## The proposal

1. **Connect Soil Health Card data to actionable advisory apps**, in
   local languages, telling a farmer exactly what and how much to apply,
   not just a lab report.
2. **Subsidised variable-rate equipment access through FPOs**, so
   smallholders can act on precision data without individual capital
   outlay.
3. **Satellite- and drone-based crop monitoring** feeding directly back
   into the advisory loop, closing it from data to action to outcome.
4. **Measure water and fertiliser savings publicly**, the metric that
   proves the rollout is working.

Ask: the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare to connect Soil Health
Card data to a funded, FPO-delivered precision-agriculture advisory and
equipment programme.
