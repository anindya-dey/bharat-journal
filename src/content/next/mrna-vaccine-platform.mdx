## Where I saw it

Moderna and BioNTech didn't just build one vaccine — they built a reusable
mRNA platform that could be re-pointed at a new pathogen within months.
That platform is now being aimed at cancer vaccines and rare-disease
treatments too. The next pandemic, or the next cancer breakthrough, will
likely come from whoever owns a platform like this.

## What's missing

India proved it can manufacture vaccines at the largest scale in the world
during COVID-19, but that was mostly viral-vector and inactivated-virus
technology. An early Indian mRNA vaccine has already been developed; the
opportunity is to turn that single product into a standing platform.

## What India already has

- An **Indian-developed mRNA COVID vaccine**, already demonstrating the
  country can design and produce this technology.
- The **world's largest vaccine-manufacturing base** by volume, led by
  Serum Institute and other major Indian manufacturers.
- **ICMR and the Department of Biotechnology's BIRAC** already fund
  biotech research and translation.

## The proposal

1. **A national mRNA platform programme** — not a single vaccine, but a
   reusable design-and-manufacture capability that can be re-targeted
   quickly.
2. **Cold-chain-light formulations** as a research priority, since India's
   last-mile distribution needs vaccines that survive heat better than the
   first generation did.
3. **Extend the platform to cancer and rare-disease vaccines**, where mRNA
   research is moving fastest globally.
4. **A pandemic-readiness stockpile model**, so the platform can be
   activated within months of a new pathogen being identified.

Ask: the Department of Biotechnology and ICMR to fund a national mRNA
platform programme, building on India's existing vaccine and manufacturing
base.
