## Where I saw it

In-situ resource utilisation — extracting water, oxygen or fuel from
material already on another world, instead of carrying everything from
Earth — is the technology that turns a visit into a sustainable presence.
Every major space agency researches it; none has yet landed and run a
working extraction demonstrator on the Moon.

## What's missing

India's Chandrayaan missions found evidence of water-ice near the lunar
south pole and proved India can land precisely in that exact region. What
hasn't happened yet, by India or anyone else, is a lander that actually
extracts and processes that water into something usable — the step between
discovery and resource.

## What India already has

- **Chandrayaan-3's precision landing** near the lunar south pole, the
  exact region where water-ice evidence has been found.
- **India's deep materials-processing and chemical-engineering base**,
  relevant to designing a compact in-situ extraction unit.
- **The Bharatiya Antariksh Station and lunar-landing roadmap**, giving a
  prospecting mission a clear purpose within a larger programme.

## The proposal

1. **A small lunar ISRU demonstrator lander**, targeting the same
   south-pole region as Chandrayaan-3, to extract and measure water from
   the regolith.
2. **Design for refuelling and life-support applications**, so a
   successful demonstrator feeds directly into the human-lunar-landing
   roadmap.
3. **International science partnership on instruments**, while keeping
   the lander and extraction hardware Indian-built.
4. **Treat it as the first resource claim of a new era**, not just a
   science mission — the first country to extract usable material from
   another world sets the precedent others follow.

Ask: ISRO to commission a lunar in-situ resource utilisation demonstrator
mission to the south-pole region.
