## Where I saw it

Singapore's courts run almost entirely online, from e-filing to virtual
hearings, and resolve most civil cases in months. Estonia's e-File system
lets lawyers and judges work from one digital case record end to end. India
built the world's largest digital identity and payments systems — but its
courts still run on paper case files and decades-long backlogs.

## What's missing

India's pending-case backlog runs into the tens of millions, and the
bottleneck is as much process as it is judges. The opportunity is to apply
India's own digital-public-infrastructure playbook — the one that worked for
banking and welfare — to the judiciary.

## What India already has

- **e-Courts Mission Mode Project** already digitises case filing and
  records in many courts.
- **Virtual hearings**, used widely during the pandemic, proved Indian
  courts can function online at scale.
- **[DigiLocker](https://www.digilocker.gov.in/) and Aadhaar e-KYC** give the identity and document
  verification layer for a fully digital case file.

## The proposal

1. **AI-assisted case triage** — cluster similar cases, flag the ones ready
   for summary disposal, and route complex matters to the right bench
   faster.
2. **A single digital case record** per matter, end to end, replacing paper
   files across every court tier.
3. **Default virtual hearings** for procedural matters, reserving physical
   courtrooms for matters that need them.
4. **A public backlog dashboard**, so reform is measured against a visible
   number, the way UPI's own metrics are published.

Ask: the Ministry of Law & Justice and the e-Courts Mission to pilot AI case
triage in the district courts with the largest backlogs.
