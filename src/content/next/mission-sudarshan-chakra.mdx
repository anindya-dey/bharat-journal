## Where I saw it

Israel's layered air-defence system hands off a threat automatically
between short-range interceptors, medium-range systems and long-range
exo-atmospheric interceptors, all fed by one shared radar and command
picture — no single layer has to cover every threat, the system as a
whole does. India has already committed to building its own version of
exactly this, under the name Mission Sudarshan Chakra, and has
publicly set a long-term target to complete it nationwide.

## What's missing

The individual layers — Akash, QRSAM, the Russian S-400, and shorter-range
guns — already exist and were proven in recent operational use. What's
still being built is the fused, automatically-handing-off command picture
across all of them, and the extension of that coverage to every part of
the country rather than its most critical sites first.

## What India already has

- **Mission Sudarshan Chakra**, the government's own name for a planned,
  AI-driven, multi-layered national air-defence shield, announced
  following recent operational experience and targeted for completion
  within about a decade.
- **A proven four-layer architecture already in service** — L-70-class
  guns for the closest range, Akash and QRSAM for short-to-medium range,
  and the S-400 ("Sudarshan Chakra" system) for long range and high
  altitude.
- **"Chakravyuh"**, the informal name used widely in Indian media and
  military commentary for this same layered, hard-to-escape air-defence
  concept — a reference to the multi-ringed battle formation from the
  Mahabharata.
- **DRDO's Ballistic Missile Defence programme**, already tested against
  ballistic-missile-class targets, a further layer the mission draws on.

## The ask

1. **Deliver the unified command-and-control fusion layer** — the piece
   that turns four separately-sourced systems into one automatically
   handing-off shield — on the timeline already announced.
2. **Close the low-altitude drone-and-rocket gap first**, the threat
   class that has most tested less-integrated air defences elsewhere in
   recent years.
3. **Indigenise every layer over time**, reducing dependence on any single
   foreign supplier for spares or upgrades as the mission matures.
4. **Publish a transparent, public rollout order** — which regions and
   installations are covered first and when — so the mission's progress
   can be tracked the way other national missions are.
