## Where I saw it

Israel's Iron Beam and similar programmes in the US and UK point to a
future where cheap drone and rocket swarms are shot down with a
laser that costs a fraction of an interceptor missile per shot, and never
runs out of ammunition as long as there's power. Against the low-cost drone
threats that have reshaped recent conflicts, cost-per-shot is the whole
battle.

## What's missing

Low-cost drones are now a frontline threat that expensive missile
interceptors are a poor economic match for — a $50,000 drone forcing a
$500,000 missile response is not a sustainable exchange rate. India has
already demonstrated laser-weapon prototypes; the opportunity is to move
from demonstration to fielded, networked point-defence.

## What India already has

- **DRDO's directed-energy weapon programme**, which has already
  demonstrated laser systems capable of engaging drone and fixed-wing
  targets at short range.
- **India's solar and power-electronics base**, relevant to the high-power
  systems a fielded laser weapon needs.
- **A clear and growing threat case** — drone incursions along India's
  borders and over sensitive sites.

## The proposal

1. **Move from demonstrator to fielded units**, starting with point
   defence of airbases and other high-value sites.
2. **Network laser defence with radar and command systems**, so it slots
   into the broader missile-shield architecture already proposed.
3. **A parallel high-power microwave programme**, for swarms a single-beam
   laser can't engage fast enough.
4. **Publish a directed-energy roadmap**, so industry can plan supply
   chains around a fielding timeline rather than a research budget line.

Ask: DRDO to publish a fielding timeline for directed-energy point-defence
systems, starting with airbase protection.
