## Where I saw it

Israel's Iron Beam and similar programmes in the US and UK point to a
future where cheap drone and rocket swarms are shot down with a
laser that costs a fraction of an interceptor missile per shot, and never
runs out of ammunition as long as there's power. Against the low-cost
drone threats that have reshaped recent conflicts, cost-per-shot is the
whole battle, and India has already proven it can build one of these.

## What's missing

Low-cost drones are now a frontline threat that expensive missile
interceptors are a poor economic match for — a cheap drone forcing an
expensive missile response is not a sustainable exchange rate. DRDO has
already field-tested a 30-kilowatt laser weapon successfully against
aerial targets and begun transferring the technology to industry; what's
missing is the step from a successful trial to fielded units at scale.

## What India already has

- **DRDO's Mk-II(A) Laser Directed-Energy Weapon**, a vehicle-mounted
  30-kilowatt system field-tested successfully in 2025 against drone and
  fixed-wing targets, putting India among a small group of nations with
  this capability demonstrated.
- **Technology transfer to the private sector already underway**, so
  production can scale beyond a single DRDO-built prototype.
- **India's solar and power-electronics base**, relevant to the
  high-power systems a fielded laser weapon needs.
- **A clear and growing threat case** — drone incursions along India's
  borders and over sensitive sites.

1. **Move from technology transfer to fielded units**, starting with
   point defence of airbases and other high-value sites.
2. **Network laser defence with radar and command systems**, so it slots
   into Mission Sudarshan Chakra's broader layered-shield architecture.
3. **A parallel high-power microwave programme**, for swarms a
   single-beam laser can't engage fast enough.
4. **Publish a public fielding timeline**, so industry can plan supply
   chains around production volumes rather than a single prototype.
