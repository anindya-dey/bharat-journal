## Where I saw it

Spain has run the world's highest deceased-organ-donation rate for decades,
not through advertising but through a system design choice: donation after
death is presumed unless a person has registered otherwise, paired with
specially trained hospital transplant coordinators. The UK, several other
European countries, and some US states have since adopted similar opt-out
models.

## What's missing

India's transplant waiting lists run into the hundreds of thousands, and the
gap between organs needed and organs donated is large. India's system is
opt-in, awareness is low, and hospital-level coordination is inconsistent
across states.

## What India already has

- **NOTTO (National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation)** already runs
  a national registry and allocation system.
- **[Aadhaar](https://uidai.gov.in/)** gives a ready identity layer for an opt-out registry linked to
  every citizen.
- A **living-donor transplant programme** that already performs large
  volumes of kidney and liver transplants.

## The proposal

1. **A national opt-out donation registry**, linked to Aadhaar, with a
   simple way to register a refusal — the Spanish default, built on Indian
   rails.
2. **Trained transplant coordinators** in every large hospital, modelled on
   Spain's hospital-coordinator network, which does more to raise donation
   rates than any campaign.
3. **A public donation and waiting-list dashboard**, so progress is visible
   and trusted.
4. **Faith and community engagement** built in from the start, since consent
   norms vary across India's communities.

Ask: the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare and NOTTO to pilot an opt-out
registry in a few states with strong hospital networks already in place.
