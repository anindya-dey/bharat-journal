## Where I saw it

Israel's layered air-defence system hands off a threat automatically
between short-range interceptors, medium-range systems and long-range
exo-atmospheric interceptors, all fed by one shared radar and command
picture. No single layer has to cover every threat — the system as a whole
does.

## What's missing

India operates capable systems at multiple ranges — Akash, QRSAM and the
Russian S-400 among them — but they were acquired at different times from
different sources, and the opportunity is to fuse them into one integrated,
automatically-handing-off shield rather than a set of separate defences
each covering its own layer.

## What India already has

- **Akash and QRSAM**, indigenous short- and quick-reaction air-defence
  missile systems already in service.
- **The S-400 Triumf**, a long-range Russian air-defence system already
  operating within India's air-defence network.
- **DRDO's Ballistic Missile Defence programme**, already tested against
  ballistic-missile-class targets.

## The proposal

1. **A unified air-defence command-and-control layer** that fuses radar
   data across all systems into one picture, handing threats off between
   layers automatically.
2. **Close the low-altitude drone-and-rocket gap**, the exact threat class
   that overwhelmed less-integrated air defences elsewhere in recent years.
3. **Indigenous production at every layer**, reducing dependence on any
   single foreign supplier for spares or upgrades.
4. **Protect critical infrastructure first** — power grids, ports and
   command centres — before extending city-wide coverage.

Ask: DRDO and the Ministry of Defence to commission an integrated air- and
missile-defence command architecture across existing systems.
