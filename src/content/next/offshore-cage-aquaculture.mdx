## Where I saw it

Norway turned offshore cage aquaculture into one of its largest export
industries, farming fish in open-water cages along its coast rather than
in ponds or tanks, with far higher yield per unit of water than
traditional methods. It transformed a fishing nation into a farming one,
without reducing wild-catch fisheries.

## What's missing

India's Blue Revolution scheme has grown aquaculture substantially, but
overwhelmingly through pond-based inland and coastal farming. Offshore
cage culture in India's open coastal waters — which could multiply yield
per hectare of water and reduce pressure on crowded pond systems — remains
at a pilot stage.

## What India already has

- **The Blue Revolution / Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana**, already
  funding aquaculture infrastructure at national scale.
- **A long coastline and exclusive economic zone**, with calm-water
  stretches well suited to offshore cages.
- **Early offshore-cage pilots**, already run by Indian fisheries research
  institutes, proving the model works in Indian waters.

## The proposal

1. **Scale offshore cage culture from pilot to funded mission**, in the
   coastal states where early trials have already succeeded.
2. **Train coastal fishing communities as the operators**, so the
   industry builds on existing livelihoods rather than displacing them.
3. **Export-grade quality and cold-chain standards from the start**,
   learning from Norway's traceability systems, to access premium export
   markets.
4. **Environmental safeguards built in**, since cage density and
   placement determine whether coastal ecosystems benefit or suffer.

Ask: the Department of Fisheries to fund a national offshore cage-
aquaculture scale-up under the existing Blue Revolution scheme.
