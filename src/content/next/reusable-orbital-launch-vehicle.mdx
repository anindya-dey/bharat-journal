## Where I saw it

SpaceX's Falcon 9 turned the rocket from a single-use expense into a
reusable asset, cutting the cost of reaching orbit by roughly an order of
magnitude and reshaping the entire global launch market in a decade. The
country that masters reuse captures the launch market that follows.

## What's missing

ISRO has already run Reusable Launch Vehicle technology demonstrations,
including autonomous landing experiments. What's missing is the step from
demonstrator to an operational, orbital-class rocket that lands, is
refurbished, and flies again — the step that actually changes launch
economics rather than just proving the physics.

## What India already has

- **ISRO's RLV-TD and Pushpak landing experiments**, already demonstrating
  autonomous runway landing from a released test vehicle.
- **PSLV, GSLV and LVM3**, a mature expendable launch-vehicle family to
  build reuse technology onto.
- **A fast-growing private launch sector** — Skyroot and Agnikul are
  already flying their own vehicles, a competitive base reuse can draw on.

## The proposal

1. **Fund the step from demonstrator to orbital-class reusable rocket**,
   with a fixed target date, not an open-ended research line.
2. **Open the programme to private partners**, so Skyroot- and Agnikul-
   class companies can compete for reuse contracts rather than ISRO
   building it alone.
3. **Target a landing-and-refly cadence**, not just a single successful
   landing, since the economics only work with rapid turnaround.
4. **Publish a cost-per-kilogram target**, so progress is measured against
   the metric that actually matters commercially.

Ask: ISRO to commit to a funded, dated path from RLV-TD to an operational
reusable orbital launch vehicle.
