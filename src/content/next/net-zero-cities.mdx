## Where I saw it

The cities of the future are being designed now — and the countries that get
the template right will export it. India's urbanisation is the largest
construction programme in the world; doing it net-zero is both a climate
necessity and an industrial opportunity.

## What's missing

India has committed to net-zero by 2070 and has some of the world's cheapest
renewable power. The opportunity is to apply both to *new* cities and
districts, so that growth and emissions are decoupled from the start rather
than retrofitted decades later.

## What India already has

- **India's solar build-out** now makes renewables over half of installed
  capacity — the clean-energy input is ready.
- **Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (GIFT City)** and **Amaravati** show
  India building planned greenfield districts.
- **Energy-conservation building codes (ECBC)** already exist for large
  commercial buildings.

## The proposal

1. **Net-zero as the default for new cities and greenfield districts** —
   renewable-powered, transit-oriented, walkable, with district cooling and
   green building standards baked in.
2. **A net-zero building code** extended to all new construction, residential
   and commercial.
3. **District energy systems** — shared cooling, rooftop solar, and
   battery/green-hydrogen storage at the neighbourhood scale.
4. **Retrofit the worst first** — target the most energy-intensive existing
   districts with efficiency upgrades.

Ask: MoHUA and the Bureau of Energy Efficiency to make net-zero the default
standard for all new greenfield urban development.
