## The Spark

In Korea, tourists don't just see the country — they collect it. Stamp trails at palaces,
temples, and observation towers; region-themed stamp books; a "passport" that fills
up with every place visited. Japan's shrine-and-temple stamp culture (*goshuin*) has
run for centuries and now sells out reprint volumes. The mechanism is ancient:
proof of visit, made beautiful, made collectible.

## The Gap

India receives millions of foreign tourists and one of the world's largest
domestic travel flows, yet each visit stands on its own — a ticket, a photo,
a memory. The opportunity is to connect the dots: a single collectible thread
that links the Taj Mahal to Konark to Hampi and gently pulls a traveller
toward the next stop. The National Tourism Policy's threads — destination
branding, digital tourism — are the perfect home for a gamified layer.

## The Precedent

India already has the fragments: temple *prasad* bands and shrine tokens,
pilgrimage registration systems (Char Dham), state-level "tourist police"
and monument audio guides. Digitally, India has the world's best public
rails for identity and payments. What's missing is the design decision to
combine them.

## The Proposal

1. **A physical "Bharat Passport"** — a designed booklet, sold at ASI monuments,
   airports, and hotel desks, with designated stamp points at 500+ monuments,
   museums, national parks, and cultural sites.
2. **A digital twin** — scan a QR at each site, get a verifiable digital stamp
   in a DigiLocker-stored "Travel Ledger"; stats, badges, and shareable
   collections.
3. **Tiers and rewards** — state-level completions ("All 12 Jyotirlingas"),
   circuit completions (Ganga towns, Buddhist circuit), and annual limited
   editions (Republic Day, state tourism years).
4. **Retail ecosystem** — partner with state emporia and One District One
   Product for location-linked souvenirs: each stamp page advertises the
   local craft.

The ask to the Ministry of Tourism: **pilot it on one circuit** (say, Delhi–Agra–Jaipur
or the Buddhist circuit) within two tourism seasons, with ASI, NPCI (for payments)
and DigiLocker (for the digital twin) at the table.
