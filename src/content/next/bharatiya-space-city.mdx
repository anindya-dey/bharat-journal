## Where I saw it

Every orbital outpost built so far — the ISS, China's Tiangong, and
India's own planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station — is a research station:
a handful of modules, a rotating crew, a science mission. No country has
yet built a larger, semi-permanent orbital settlement with the scale,
redundancy and purpose of a small city — for research, manufacturing and
strategic presence combined. It is a genuinely open frontier.

## What's missing

India's space station is targeted for the mid-2030s and is, by design, a
research outpost of modest scale. The opportunity is to use it as the
first module of something larger, and to say so now — a long-horizon plan
for an expanded, multi-purpose orbital settlement, rather than treating
the station as the final destination.

## What India already has

- **The Bharatiya Antariksh Station**, planned for the mid-2030s, the
  literal first building block.
- **Gaganyaan**, proving crewed-spaceflight capability.
- **A growing private space-manufacturing and launch sector**, which an
  expanded settlement would need to supply and service it affordably.

## The proposal

1. **Publish a long-horizon expansion concept** for the Bharatiya
   Antariksh Station — additional modules for research, manufacturing and
   strategic communications — beyond its initial research-station scope.
2. **Design for growth from the start**, so early station modules are
   compatible with later expansion rather than a dead end.
3. **Separate civilian research and strategic-use modules clearly**, so
   the settlement's dual purpose is transparent rather than ambiguous.
4. **Treat it as the staging base** for the lunar-landing roadmap and
   future deep-space missions, not a standalone programme.

Ask: ISRO, with the Defence Space Agency, to publish a long-horizon concept
for expanding the Bharatiya Antariksh Station into a larger, multi-purpose
orbital settlement.
