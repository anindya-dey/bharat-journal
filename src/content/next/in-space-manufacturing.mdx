## Where I saw it

A US space-manufacturing startup is already flying capsules that produce
specialty fibre optics and pharmaceutical protein crystals in microgravity
— materials that form with fewer defects in orbit than they can on Earth.
It's one of the first genuinely commercial uses of space beyond
communications and imaging, and almost no one else is doing it yet.

## What's missing

India has deep strengths in both space hardware and materials science and
pharmaceuticals, but no dedicated in-space manufacturing programme
connecting them. The gap is less about capability than about treating
orbit as a manufacturing environment rather than only a platform for
satellites.

## What India already has

- **ISRO's microgravity research experience**, including past
  materials-science experiments flown on earlier missions.
- **India's pharmaceutical and fibre-optic manufacturing industries**,
  the exact sectors this technology could serve first.
- **IN-SPACe and a growing private space sector**, able to license and
  operate a commercial manufacturing capsule.

## The proposal

1. **Fund a small returnable-capsule demonstrator**, testing one or two
   high-value materials — a specialty pharmaceutical crystal or optical
   fibre — in microgravity.
2. **Partner with Indian pharmaceutical and materials companies** as the
   paying customers from day one, rather than treating this as pure
   research.
3. **Build on the Bharatiya Antariksh Station's capability** for
   longer-duration manufacturing experiments once it's operational.
4. **License it through IN-SPACe** as a private venture, with ISRO
   providing launch and recovery support.

Ask: ISRO and IN-SPACe to fund a small in-space manufacturing demonstrator
with an Indian pharmaceutical or materials partner.
