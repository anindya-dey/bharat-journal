## Where I saw it

In Japan, a phone buzzes with a countdown before the ground moves. Mexico's
SASMEX has warned Mexico City for over 30 years. California's ShakeAlert does
the same across the US West Coast. These systems don't predict earthquakes —
they detect the first, harmless waves and race the destructive ones to warn
people first.

## What's missing

The Himalayan arc and large parts of the Northeast sit in the highest seismic
zones on Earth, and Delhi, the Kashmir valley and the Northeast are all
overdue for a major quake by historical recurrence patterns. India has strong
seismology research but no public early-warning system that reaches phones,
trains and schools before the shaking arrives.

## What India already has

- The **National Centre for Seismology (NCS)** already runs a dense national
  seismic monitoring network.
- India has built **mass public alert rails** before — the Common Alerting
  Protocol used for cyclone and flood warnings, and the same SMS/cell
  broadcast infrastructure used in disaster alerts.
- **Research collaborations** with Japan and the US on seismology already
  exist.

## The proposal

1. **A public earthquake early-warning system**, modelled on ShakeAlert and
   SASMEX, for the Himalayan belt and the Northeast first.
2. **Automatic safety triggers** — slow trains, halt metro systems, and open
   elevator doors automatically on a warning, the way Japan's rail network
   does.
3. **School and hospital drills** tied to the same alert, so the warning
   translates into action, not just notification.
4. **Open sensor data**, so research institutions and private apps can build
   on the network rather than wait for one app to work.

Ask: the Ministry of Earth Sciences and NDMA to commission a pilot
early-warning zone across Delhi-NCR and the Northeast within one seismic
monitoring cycle.
