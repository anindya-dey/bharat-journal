## Where I saw it

The UK's auto-enrolment reform and the US's growing use of auto-enrol 401(k)
plans both found the same thing: people save far more when saving is the
default and opting out takes effort, than when saving requires an active
choice. Coverage rose sharply within a few years in both countries without
making saving mandatory.

## What's missing

India's National Pension System and EPFO cover government and larger
formal-sector employees well, but the much larger informal and gig
workforce has low voluntary pension coverage. The country that built Jan
Dhan and UPI to default people into financial inclusion hasn't yet applied
the same default-design thinking to retirement saving.

## What India already has

- **NPS and EPFO**, already running pension infrastructure at national
  scale for the formal sector.
- **[Jan Dhan accounts](https://pmjdy.gov.in/)**, reaching nearly every household, which already
  carry the banking rail auto-contributions would use.
- **[UPI AutoPay](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi)**, which already supports recurring, consent-based debits —
  the exact mechanism auto-enrolment needs.

## The proposal

1. **Default enrolment into NPS** for gig and informal workers registered
   through platforms like e-Shram, with a simple opt-out rather than an
   opt-in form.
2. **Small automatic contributions via UPI AutoPay**, scaled to income, so
   saving happens without an active monthly decision.
3. **Portable accounts** that follow a worker between gig platforms and
   formal jobs, so pension saving isn't reset by a job change.
4. **A matching incentive** for the lowest-income savers, to make the
   default genuinely worth keeping.

Ask: the PFRDA and Ministry of Labour to pilot auto-enrolment through
e-Shram and gig-platform payrolls.
