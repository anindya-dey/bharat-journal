## Where I saw it

NASA's Artemis programme and China's lunar ambitions both talk about a
sustained lunar presence, but no country has yet built and permanently
crewed a lunar surface base — the Moon, more than fifty years after Apollo,
still has no residents. Whoever builds the first permanent outpost sets the
template — and likely the real-estate claims — for everyone after.

## What's missing

India's roadmap, once a human lunar landing is achieved, has no published
next step toward an actual outpost. The south-pole water-ice evidence
Chandrayaan found is exactly the resource a permanent base would need for
water, oxygen and fuel — the opportunity is to plan the outpost around
that discovery now, rather than treating the landing itself as the finish
line.

## What India already has

- **Chandrayaan-3's south-pole landing**, in the exact region best suited
  to a resource-supported outpost.
- **The lunar ISRU resource-prospecting mission** already proposed,
  which this outpost would be built on top of.
- **India's Antarctic and Arctic research stations**, decades of
  experience running permanently staffed outposts in extreme, isolated
  environments — the closest Earth-based analogue to a lunar base.

## The proposal

1. **Design the outpost around the ISRU demonstrator's findings**, so
   water extraction is built into the base from its first module, not
   added later.
2. **Start with short-duration crewed rotations**, extending gradually —
   the same phased approach India's polar stations already use.
3. **International partnership on science modules**, while India leads
   and builds the habitat and life-support core.
4. **Publish a decades-long but concrete staffing and expansion plan**,
   so "permanent" is a funded commitment, not a slogan.

Ask: ISRO to publish a phased plan for a permanently staffed lunar outpost,
sequenced after the ISRU resource-prospecting and human-landing missions.
