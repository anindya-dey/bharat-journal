## Where I saw it

No country has done more with less in space than India. Mangalyaan reached
Mars orbit on its first attempt for less than a Hollywood budget; Chandrayaan-3
touched down near the lunar south pole in 2023, a world first; Aditya-L1 is
studying the Sun. India has earned the right to aim higher.

## What's missing

India is a top-tier spacefaring nation with a small fraction of the
commercial space economy. The opportunity is to convert scientific
achievement into an industrial ecosystem — human spaceflight, a national
station, and a private launch and satellite industry.

## What India already has

- **Gaganyaan** — India's human spaceflight programme, in development.
- **Bharatiya Antariksh Station** — a planned Indian space station, targeted
  for the mid-2030s.
- **IN-SPACe** (2020) opened the sector to private players; **Skyroot**,
  **Agnikul** and **Pixxel** are already launching.
- **Chandrayaan-3** and **Aditya-L1** proved deep-space capability.

## Already in motion

The government has already committed to this: Gaganyaan is in development, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station is targeted for the mid-2030s, and the 2020 space reforms opened the sector to private players. Here's what's underway:

## The ask

1. **Deliver Gaganyaan and the Bharatiya Antariksh Station** on schedule, with
   a steady human-spaceflight cadence.
2. **Build a reusable launch vehicle** to cut the cost of access to orbit.
3. **Grow the private space economy** — launch, satellites, and applications —
   toward a multi-fold increase in India's share of the global market.
4. **Aim for a lunar south-pole research outpost**, building on Chandrayaan's
   discoveries and the water-ice it found.
