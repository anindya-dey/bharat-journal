## The Spark

The Arctic is warming several times faster than the rest of the planet, and
melting ice is opening new sea routes and resource frontiers. Nations far from
the Arctic — including India — are building polar science programmes to
understand and help govern the change.

## The Gap

India already has an Arctic research station and Antarctic bases, but its polar
programme is small relative to the stakes. The opportunity is to deepen the
science, the technology and the diplomatic presence, so India helps shape polar
governance rather than reacting to it.

## The Precedent

- **Himadri** — India's Arctic research station at Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard.
- **Dakshin Gangotri and Maitri** — India's long-running Antarctic stations.
- India became a **consultative member of the Antarctic Treaty** system and an
  **observer in the Arctic Council**.

## The Proposal

1. **Expand polar science** — a larger sustained research presence, with
   focus on climate modelling that affects Indian monsoons and coasts.
2. **Polar technology** — ice-capable vessels, cold-climate instruments, and
   satellite monitoring, as an Indian capability.
3. **Strengthen governance participation** — a more prominent role in Antarctic
   and Arctic bodies as the rules evolve.
4. **A polar-climate data programme** — feeding directly into India's own
   climate resilience and water security.

Ask: the Ministry of Earth Sciences and MEA to expand India's polar programme
and governance role.
