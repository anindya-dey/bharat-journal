## Where I saw it

Singapore's Electronic Road Pricing adjusts tolls by time of day to keep
traffic flowing; London and Stockholm charge for entering the city centre at
peak hours and reinvest the revenue in transit. None of these cities banned
cars — they priced the scarce resource, which is road space at peak time.

## What's missing

India's metros already have FASTag, which solved electronic tolling on
highways. The same rail hasn't been extended to urban congestion pricing or
AI-timed traffic signals, so gridlock is managed with traffic police and
fixed-time signals rather than data.

## What India already has

- **FASTag** — a national electronic-tolling rail already used by hundreds
  of millions of vehicles, the exact infrastructure congestion pricing would
  reuse.
- **Smart traffic-signal pilots** in several cities already use AI to time
  signals by real-time flow.
- **[ONDC](https://ondc.org/)-style open-network thinking**, which has already shown India can build
  open data layers for mobility.

## The proposal

1. **Dynamic congestion pricing** in city centres at peak hours, charged
   through FASTag, starting in one or two of the most gridlocked business
   districts.
2. **AI-timed traffic signals** city-wide, replacing fixed-time cycles with
   real-time, camera- and sensor-driven timing.
3. **Reinvest the revenue in transit** — buses, metro expansion and
   pedestrian infrastructure — so pricing is seen as funding alternatives,
   not just charging a toll.
4. **Publish congestion data openly**, so citizens and apps can route around
   it and hold the system accountable.

Ask: the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs and state transport departments
to pilot congestion pricing in one business district within a FASTag-linked
framework.
