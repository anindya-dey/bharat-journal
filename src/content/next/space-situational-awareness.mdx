## Where I saw it

The US Space Surveillance Network tracks tens of thousands of objects in
orbit continuously, giving it independent warning of collisions and
potentially hostile satellite manoeuvres. As more countries and companies
launch into the same crowded low-earth orbit, the ability to see what's
up there — not just rely on someone else's catalogue — becomes a strategic
asset in its own right.

## What's missing

India depends substantially on shared international debris and
object-tracking data rather than an independent, dense domestic
space-surveillance network, even as its own satellite fleet and the
traffic around the Indian Ocean region grow.

## What India already has

- **ISRO's existing tracking and telemetry stations**, a network that
  already supports launch and satellite operations.
- **The Defence Space Agency**, with an institutional mandate this
  network would directly support.
- **Growing radar and optical-telescope infrastructure** across Indian
  research institutions, adaptable for space-object tracking.

## The proposal

1. **A dedicated space-situational-awareness network** — radar and
   optical sensors — covering the Indian Ocean region's orbital traffic
   independently.
2. **Fuse the data with the Defence Space Agency's counter-space mission**,
   so military and civilian tracking reinforce each other.
3. **Share non-sensitive data internationally**, building India's standing
   as a contributor to global space-safety norms, not just a consumer of
   others' tracking data.
4. **Prioritise India's own high-value satellites** for close, continuous
   monitoring first.

Ask: ISRO and the Defence Space Agency to jointly fund a dedicated
space-situational-awareness sensor network.
