## Where I saw it

In Barcelona, a tourist lands and moves: a transit card works on metro,
tram, bus and suburban rail, buyable at any airport machine, no
registration, no app account. In Basel, the hotel card *is* the transit
pass. The magic isn't the metro network — it's the seamlessness at the
edges.

## What's missing

India's transit story is strong: Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad,
Kolkata, and 40+ more cities run metros; [PM e-Bus Sewa](https://www.mohua.gov.in/) has put thousands of
e-buses on the streets; e-rickshaws solved part of the last mile on their own.
The opportunity is the **feeder layer and the single ticket** that ties it all
together:

1. **One integrated ticket** — today each mode is its own app, card, or
   purchase. A single pass for airport-to-hotel-to-monument would turn three
   research steps per leg into one tap.
2. **Feeder mapping as data** — publishing GTFS feeds for monument-adjacent
   bus and auto routes lets maps and ride-hailing apps route visitors
   precisely.
3. **The last 500 metres** — clear signage and shaded walkways from the
   nearest stop to the gate, standardised the way ASI signage already is.

## What India already has

Delhi's Metro feeds a million+ daily; Kochi's Water Metro shows
India can build beautiful integrated niche transit; ONDC has
demonstrated the *open network* playbook in mobility-adjacent retail.
NAMASTE-style mobility-as-a-service pilots exist in the National
Common Mobility Card (NCMC) — one card, many metros — but adoption
outside metro gates is thin.

## The proposal

A **"Tourist Mobility Product"** pilot in one state:

1. **One card, one purchase** — a ₹500/7-day tourist card, NCMC-based,
   valid metro + bus + (partner) e-rickshaw, sold at airports, major
   stations, hotel desks. No registration, refundable deposit.
2. **Feeder mapping as data** — publish GTFS feeds for monument-adjacent
   bus/auto routes; let Google/Uber/OLAs integrate them (open data,
   not another government app).
3. **"500-metre rule"** at monuments — signage and shaded walkways
   from the nearest transit stop to the gate, standardised like ASI
   signage.

Ask the Ministry of Tourism + MoHUA ([Smart Cities](https://smartcities.gov.in/)) to pilot in
Jaipur or Hampi (small, high-tourist-share, defensible) before
scaling to the Ayr of Agra or Varanasi.
