## Where I saw it

Extra-large unmanned underwater vehicle programmes in the US and China aim
to patrol, lay sensors, and even strike, for months at a time, without
risking a crewed submarine or its sailors. Underwater, where detection is
hardest and endurance matters most, autonomy changes the economics of
presence entirely.

## What's missing

India's undersea capability has focused on crewed submarines and the
Samudrayaan scientific submersible. A dedicated military-grade unmanned
underwater vehicle programme — for anti-submarine warfare, mine-hunting and
long-endurance surveillance — does not yet exist at comparable scale to the
countries racing ahead in this category.

## What India already has

- **The Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan (Matsya-6000)**, proving India
  can design and operate crewed deep-sea submersibles.
- **India's submarine-building programme**, including indigenous
  conventional and nuclear-powered boats, a direct source of underwater
  engineering talent.
- **DRDO's underwater-robotics and sensor research**, already underway.

## The proposal

1. **A military-grade unmanned underwater vehicle programme**, distinct
   from the scientific Samudrayaan effort, for anti-submarine warfare and
   mine-hunting.
2. **Long-endurance variants** for persistent surveillance of the Indian
   Ocean's chokepoints, where crewed-submarine presence is costly to
   sustain continuously.
3. **Shared underwater-engineering talent** between the Deep Ocean Mission
   and the Navy's programme, rather than two disconnected efforts.
4. **A seabed-sensor network** laid and maintained by these vehicles, for
   persistent undersea domain awareness.

Ask: DRDO, the Indian Navy and the Ministry of Earth Sciences to commission
a dedicated military UUV programme, sharing talent with the Deep Ocean
Mission.
