## Where I saw it

The US national laboratory system deliberately spreads expertise in any
given field — nuclear, energy, computing — across multiple labs in
different states, so a mission never depends on a single site, and so
competing teams can pursue different approaches to the same problem in
parallel. Redundancy, in this model, is treated as a feature that speeds
research up, not a cost that slows it down.

## What's missing

Several of India's flagship missions — quantum, semiconductors, advanced
reactor research — concentrate their lead institutions in one or two
cities each. That creates a single point of failure if that site is ever
disrupted, and it means there's no second team quietly pursuing a
different approach to the same hard problem.

## What India already has

- **IITs, IISc and a network of national laboratories** already spread
  across many states, a foundation to build a deliberate "second site"
  policy onto, rather than starting from nothing.
- **The National Quantum Mission's multi-hub structure already proposed**
  in this journal, a template this rule could formalise across every
  mission.
- **DRDO and ISRO's own multi-centre structures**, proof India already
  runs some missions this way — the ask is to make it the rule, not the
  exception.

## The proposal

1. **A minimum-two-site rule** for every national mission above a funding
   threshold, each site resourced enough to make real progress alone.
2. **Deliberately different approaches at each site**, not identical
   duplicate labs, so redundancy also means more chances at a
   breakthrough.
3. **Apply it retroactively** to missions that currently concentrate in
   one city, where feasible, not just to new missions going forward.
4. **Publish the policy as a standing rule**, not a case-by-case
   decision, so every future mission is designed this way by default.

Ask: NITI Aayog and DST to adopt a minimum-two-site redundancy rule for all
national research missions above a defined funding threshold.
