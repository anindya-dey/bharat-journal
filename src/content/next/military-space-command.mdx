## Where I saw it

The US elevated its space operations into a full, independent service arm
— the Space Force — recognising that satellites for navigation,
communication and early warning have become contested military
infrastructure, not just support systems. The US Space Surveillance
Network tracks tens of thousands of orbital objects continuously, giving
independent warning of collisions and hostile manoeuvres; several major
powers are building the standing interceptor capability to act on that
warning, not just a one-off demonstration of it.

## What's missing

India has already proven the hardest parts in isolation: Mission Shakti
(2019) demonstrated kinetic anti-satellite accuracy, and a tri-service
Defence Space Agency already coordinates military space operations. What
doesn't exist yet is the standing infrastructure that turns a
demonstration into a deterrent — an independent sensor network to see
what's in orbit, and a repeatable interceptor fleet to act on what it
sees — commanded by one elevated, resourced body rather than assembled
ad hoc each time.

## What India already has

- **The Defence Space Agency**, a tri-service body already coordinating
  India's military space operations.
- **Mission Shakti (2019)**, India's demonstrated kinetic anti-satellite
  capability — a single proven test, not yet a standing fleet.
- **ISRO's existing tracking and telemetry stations**, and growing radar
  and optical-telescope infrastructure across Indian research
  institutions, both adaptable for independent space-object tracking.
- **DRDO's Ballistic Missile Defence interceptors**, which share much of
  the guidance and interception technology an orbital interceptor fleet
  would need.

## The proposal

1. **Elevate the Defence Space Agency** toward a fully independent
   command, with its own budget line and career track, as satellite
   dependency grows across every service.
2. **Build an independent space-situational-awareness network** — radar
   and optical sensors covering the Indian Ocean region's orbital
   traffic — so the command isn't dependent on shared international
   tracking data for its own high-value satellites.
3. **Build a standing counter-space interceptor fleet** under the same
   command, mixing kinetic interceptors with non-kinetic options —
   jamming and dazzling — so every response isn't a debris-generating
   kinetic strike, and tested against decommissioned rather than
   operational targets to avoid repeating 2019's debris.
4. **Publish a responsible counter-space doctrine**, distinguishing
   defensive and deterrent capability from offensive escalation, to
   govern how and when both the sensor network and the interceptor fleet
   are used.
5. **Closer fusion with ISRO and private space companies**, so military
   space capability grows alongside, not separate from, India's civilian
   space economy.

Ask: the Ministry of Defence to publish a roadmap elevating the Defence
Space Agency into a fully resourced command with its own sensor network
and interceptor fleet, not a coordinating body without either.
