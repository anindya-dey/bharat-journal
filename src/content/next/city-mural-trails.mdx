## The Spark

In Bruges, tourists follow a map of murals scattered across the city's
corners — art becomes the reason to walk the unwalked street. In Lodhi
Art District, Delhi, India has already proven the model: 50+ murals by
Indian and international artists, curated by St+art India, now a
destination in its own right. In Bruges it is tourism board policy;
in Delhi it was a foundation's initiative.

## The Gap

Lodhi Art District proves the model works. The opportunity is to turn it
into a system rather than a series of one-off moments: murals that are
mapped, maintained, and marketed as a *trail* — the unit of tourism the
Bruges model sells, and the format that turns a city walk into a
destination.

## The Precedent

- **Lodhi Art District** (Delhi, since ~2015): 50+ murals, curated,
  walkable, photographed by millions.
- **Kochi-Muziris Biennale** — the largest art event in India, proving
  art tourism has national pulling power.
- **Sassoon Dock (Mumbai), St+art's other projects** — walls as
  destination infrastructure.

## The Proposal

**A National Mural Trail Programme** under the Ministry of Tourism +
Smart Cities:

1. **One commissioned trail per state capital** — 20-40 murals per
   city, local artists first, on a mapped, signed, walkable route
   linking existing monuments (the trail *is* the new attraction
   cost-effectively).
2. **Murals as infrastructure** — anti-graffiti coating, maintenance
   budgets, LED night-lighting where appropriate; walls are owned
   by the municipality, not the artist.
3. **The collectible hook** — each trail integrates with the Tourist
   Passport (see related idea): stamp points at the top 5 murals.
4. **Artists' economy** — per-mural fees + a small royalty when
   merchandise/reprints sell, making muralism a profession, not a
   favour.

Target: pilot in 10 cities (one per zone — Panaji, Pondicherry,
Bhopal, Shillong, Jodhpur, Kochi, Mysuru, Varanasi, Chandigarh,
Visakhapatnam — cities with walkable historic cores).
