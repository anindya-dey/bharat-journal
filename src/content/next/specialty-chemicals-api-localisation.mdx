## Where I saw it

India earned the title "pharmacy of the world" by manufacturing generic
medicines at a scale and price almost no one else can match. But a
significant share of the active pharmaceutical ingredients and bulk-drug
intermediates behind those medicines is imported from one dominant
supplier — meaning the final product is Indian, but a critical input
upstream of it isn't.

## What's missing

India's PLI scheme for bulk drugs already targets a defined list of
critical API intermediates, but the opportunity is larger: extend
localisation further up the chemistry chain, into the specialty chemicals
these intermediates themselves depend on.

## What India already has

- **The PLI scheme for bulk drugs**, already funding domestic production
  of a defined set of critical API intermediates and fermentation-based
  drugs.
- **India's deep generic-pharmaceutical manufacturing base**, the
  downstream industry this localisation directly protects.
- **A growing domestic specialty-chemicals sector**, already supplying
  adjacent industries like agrochemicals and textiles.

## The proposal

1. **Extend the bulk-drug PLI list further upstream**, into the specialty
   chemicals that feed today's API intermediates, not just the
   intermediates themselves.
2. **Strategic stockpiles for the highest-risk precursors**, following
   the minimum-two-source doctrine already proposed for critical inputs.
3. **Shared testing and certification infrastructure**, so smaller
   chemical manufacturers can meet pharmaceutical-grade purity standards
   without each building their own labs.
4. **Publish an import-dependency map** for the pharmaceutical supply
   chain, the way other strategic sectors are starting to.

Ask: the Department of Pharmaceuticals to extend the bulk-drug PLI scheme
upstream into specialty-chemical precursor localisation.
