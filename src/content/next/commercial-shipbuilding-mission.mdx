## Where I saw it

South Korea, Japan and China together build the overwhelming majority of
the world's commercial ships — container vessels, tankers and bulk
carriers — a position built deliberately over decades through state
support for yards, financing and export orders. India's shipyards build
world-class naval vessels, but barely register in the commercial
shipbuilding market those three countries dominate.

## What's missing

India's defence shipyards — building frigates, destroyers and submarines —
prove the engineering and workforce capability exists. What's missing is
the deliberate extension of that capability into commercial vessels,
which could diversify the same yards' order books and build a genuine
export industry.

## What India already has

- **World-class defence shipyards**, already building advanced naval
  vessels domestically.
- **A long coastline with major ports**, already handling significant
  global shipping traffic.
- **The Maritime India Vision**, which already sets ambitions for the
  shipping and ports sector to build on.

## The proposal

1. **Dual-use shipyard expansion**, so defence yards can take on
   commercial orders in the same facilities, smoothing demand cycles for
   both.
2. **Financing support for ship buyers**, matching the export-credit
   terms Korean and Japanese yards already offer their customers.
3. **Target mid-sized commercial vessels first** — the segment where
   India's cost base is most competitive against the established
   leaders.
4. **Tie it to the broader green-shipping-fuel ambition**, so new Indian-
   built ships are positioned for the coming shift to cleaner marine
   fuels.

Ask: the Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways to publish a commercial
shipbuilding mission building on India's existing defence shipyard
capability.
