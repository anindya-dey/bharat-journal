## Where I saw it

Germany, Japan and Taiwan dominate the global machine-tools industry — the
precision lathes, mills and CNC machines that are used to build every
other piece of machinery, from cars to aircraft to other machine tools
themselves. It's sometimes called the "mother machine" industry for
exactly that reason: without it, nothing else gets built domestically
either.

## What's missing

India's defence-manufacturing corridors, semiconductor fabs and EV
factories all ultimately depend on precision machine tools, and India
imports a large share of the higher-precision ones it uses. Every other
manufacturing mission in this journal rests on this one, less visible,
layer underneath it.

## What India already has

- **An established domestic machine-tool industry**, with manufacturers
  already producing mid-tier CNC machines and tooling.
- **HMT and private-sector machine-tool makers**, decades of institutional
  experience to build higher-precision capability onto.
- **Growing demand from the defence, semiconductor and EV manufacturing
  pushes already underway**, a captive domestic market for better
  machine tools.

## The proposal

1. **A dedicated precision-machine-tools mission**, treating it as
   foundational infrastructure for every other manufacturing goal, not a
   standalone sector.
2. **Technology-transfer partnerships** with established machine-tool
   nations, structured for genuine capability transfer.
3. **Public testbeds and shared precision-metrology labs**, so smaller
   Indian toolmakers can validate against world-class standards.
4. **Tie incentives to the defence corridors, semiconductor fabs and EV
   plants already being built**, so demand and supply grow together.

Ask: the Ministry of Heavy Industries to publish a precision-machine-tools
mission tied explicitly to India's other manufacturing pushes.
