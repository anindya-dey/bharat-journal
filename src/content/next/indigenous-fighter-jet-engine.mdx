## Where I saw it

The US, France, the UK and Russia all guard fighter-jet engine technology
more closely than almost any other defence capability, because it is the
hardest part to master and the easiest point of leverage over a buyer.
India designs and builds its own fighter airframes — and has now signed
a government-to-government deal meant to finally build the engine at
home too.

## What's missing

India has built the Tejas and is developing the AMCA fifth-generation
fighter, both genuine indigenous design achievements, but every one of
them has flown on a GE or Safran engine. The deal to close that gap is
now signed; what's missing is seeing it through to a flying engine on the
timeline promised, with technology transfer that is real rather than
nominal.

## What India already has

- **A government-to-government deal with France's Safran**, announced in
  2025, to co-develop a high-thrust engine for the AMCA with DRDO's GTRE
  and HAL, reportedly valued near $7 billion and including commitments to
  full technology transfer.
- **DRDO's Kaveri engine programme**, decades of indigenous gas-turbine
  research that the new partnership builds on rather than replaces,
  including a marine and UCAV-derivative line still active.
- **HAL's dedicated new engine-assembly, testing and production
  facilities being built in Bengaluru** for the programme.

## The ask

1. **Hold the technology-transfer commitments to their word** — full
   design and manufacturing know-how, not licensed final assembly, as the
   programme matures past its early stages.
2. **Keep Kaveri's research line alive in parallel**, so India retains a
   sovereign fallback and a feed of trained engineers even as the Safran
   partnership leads.
3. **Target the AMCA's flight-test timeline** as the forcing function for
   the engine programme, with public milestones the way other national
   missions report progress.
4. **Build the testing infrastructure** — altitude test facilities,
   metallurgy labs — as sovereign capability that outlasts this one
   programme.

Ask: DRDO, HAL and the Ministry of Defence to publish public milestones
for the Safran engine partnership, tied to the AMCA programme's flight-
test timeline.
