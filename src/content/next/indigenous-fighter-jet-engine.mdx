## Where I saw it

The US, France, the UK and Russia all guard fighter-jet engine technology
more closely than almost any other defence capability, because it is the
hardest part to master and the easiest point of leverage over a buyer.
India designs and builds its own fighter airframes — but every one of them
still flies on a GE or Safran engine.

## What's missing

India has built the Tejas and is developing the AMCA fifth-generation
fighter, both genuine indigenous design achievements. But "designed in
India" still means "powered from abroad," which is a strategic dependency
on the one component a fighter programme cannot do without.

## What India already has

- **DRDO's Kaveri engine programme**, decades of indigenous gas-turbine
  research, including a marine and UCAV-derivative line still active even
  after the core Kaveri missed Tejas's requirements.
- **HAL's engine manufacturing and overhaul experience**, built over decades
  of licence-producing foreign engines.
- **Ongoing co-development talks with global engine makers**, which could
  be structured as genuine technology transfer rather than licensed
  assembly.

## The proposal

1. **A funded, time-bound indigenous combat-engine programme**, building on
   Kaveri's decades of research rather than starting over.
2. **Insist on real technology transfer**, not final-assembly licensing, in
   any foreign co-development deal.
3. **Target the AMCA's engine requirement** as the forcing function, with a
   firm date rather than an open-ended research programme.
4. **Build the testing infrastructure** — altitude test facilities,
   metallurgy labs — alongside the engine itself, so the capability
   compounds into future designs.

Ask: DRDO and HAL, with the Ministry of Defence, to publish a funded
indigenous fighter-engine roadmap tied to the AMCA programme's timeline.
