## Where I saw it

Autonomous tractors are already working fields in Australia and the US
without a driver, and robotic harvesters are picking fruit in Japan and
elsewhere, as farm labour becomes scarcer and more expensive in each of
those countries. India's own rural workforce is migrating to cities at
scale, which will eventually create the exact labour gap this technology
was built to solve.

## What's missing

India's agri-tech conversation has focused heavily on drones for spraying
and on precision-agriculture sensors, but ground-based agri-robotics —
autonomous tractors, weeding robots and harvest-assist machines suited to
Indian field sizes — remains an early-stage research area rather than a
funded national push.

## What India already has

- **A fast-growing agri-tech startup sector**, already building sensor
  and drone products for Indian farms.
- **ICAR's agricultural-engineering research network**, with existing
  mechanisation research to build autonomy onto.
- **PM Kisan Drone and similar schemes**, proof India can fund and scale
  a specific agri-robotics category quickly once it commits.

## The proposal

1. **A funded agri-robotics research mission**, focused on autonomous
   platforms sized for India's typically smaller field plots, not
   adaptations of large-farm Western equipment.
2. **Shared-ownership models through FPOs**, since autonomous equipment
   is unaffordable for most individual smallholders alone.
3. **Start with the most labour-intensive crops** — fruit and vegetable
   harvesting and weeding — where the labour gap bites hardest.
4. **Pair with the agri-robotics manufacturing base** India's own
   engineering colleges and startups could build, rather than relying on
   imports.

Ask: the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare and ICAR to fund an
agri-robotics research mission sized for Indian field plots.
