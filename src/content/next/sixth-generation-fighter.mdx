## Where I saw it

The US's Next Generation Air Dominance programme, and the UK-Italy-Japan
Global Combat Air Programme and France-Germany-Spain Future Combat Air
System, are all designing sixth-generation fighters built around networked
sensor fusion and unmanned-aircraft teaming, years before their current
fifth-generation jets even enter service. Designing the next generation
early is how a country stays in the race rather than catching up to it.

## What's missing

India's AMCA programme is a genuine fifth-generation achievement still in
development. Without a parallel, even if modest, sixth-generation research
track now, India risks finishing AMCA only to find the frontier has already
moved — the position it was in with fourth-generation jets a generation
ago.

## What India already has

- **AMCA**, India's indigenous fifth-generation fighter programme, under
  development by DRDO's Aeronautical Development Agency and HAL.
- **CATS Warrior and India's swarm-drone research**, directly relevant to
  the unmanned-teaming concepts sixth-generation designs are built around.
- **A deepening base of aerospace research institutions** across IITs and
  DRDO labs.

## The proposal

1. **A sixth-generation concept study**, run in parallel with AMCA
   development, not after it — focused on unmanned teaming, networked
   sensors and reduced pilot workload.
2. **International partnership exploration**, on India's terms, with
   consortia already defining the category.
3. **Invest in the enabling technologies now** — AI mission systems, sensor
   fusion and directed-energy self-defence — so they're mature when the
   airframe programme starts in earnest.
4. **Treat AMCA's production run as a bridge**, not an endpoint, in public
   messaging and budget planning alike.

Ask: DRDO and ADA to commission a sixth-generation concept study alongside
AMCA's ongoing development.
