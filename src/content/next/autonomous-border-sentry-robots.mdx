## Where I saw it

Israel operates armed and sensor-laden unmanned ground vehicles along
sections of its border fences, handling patrol and surveillance in terrain
where posting soldiers around the clock is costly and dangerous. The
principle generalises well beyond any one border: machines should take the
most routine and most hostile terrain first.

## What's missing

India's Himalayan and high-altitude borders are among the most
physically punishing deployed terrain in the world — extreme cold, low
oxygen, and difficult resupply. That is exactly the environment where
unmanned ground vehicles do the most good and where India has the least
fielded capability relative to its geography.

## What India already has

- **DRDO's unmanned ground vehicle research**, including robotic mules and
  surveillance platforms already demonstrated for high-altitude use.
- **India's robotics and drone startup ecosystem**, growing quickly since
  the 2021 Drone Rules.
- **Decades of high-altitude logistics experience** — the world's highest
  battlefield infrastructure already exists; sentry robots extend it.

## The proposal

1. **Fielded autonomous sentry and resupply robots** for the highest-
   altitude, lowest-density border posts first, where the human cost of
   routine patrol is greatest.
2. **AI-assisted surveillance fused with the robots**, so a single human
   operator can monitor a wide sector rather than walk it.
3. **Cold-weather-hardened design** as a distinct research track, since
   this terrain is harsher than most countries' border-robotics programmes
   are built for.
4. **A phased human-machine handover doctrine**, so robots take the
   routine load while decisions stay with soldiers.

Ask: DRDO and the Army to commission a fielded pilot of autonomous sentry
and resupply robots on one high-altitude sector.
