## Where I saw it

Making Mars habitable — thickening its atmosphere, warming its surface,
restoring a water cycle — has been discussed in scientific papers for
decades, but no space agency or company has ever funded a dedicated,
sustained terraforming research programme. It remains entirely open
scientific territory, further from being "someone else's lead" than
almost any other space ambition.

## What's missing

Indian scientists already contribute to global climate and planetary
science; what's missing is a dedicated research initiative that treats
Mars habitability as a long-horizon but serious scientific question, built
on the same climate-modelling expertise India already applies to its own
monsoon and water systems.

## What India already has

- **A deep climate-science and atmospheric-modelling community**, already
  applied to India's own monsoon forecasting.
- **Mangalyaan and India's existing Mars-orbit data**, a direct
  observational base to build terraforming research on.
- **ISRO and DST's joint planetary-science programmes**, an existing
  structure to house a long-horizon research line.

## The proposal

1. **A long-horizon, modestly funded Mars-habitability research
   programme**, explicitly framed as multi-decade basic science, not a
   near-term engineering project.
2. **Use Mangalyaan-class orbiters to gather the atmospheric data**
   terraforming models need — pressure, composition, and radiation over
   time.
3. **Publish research openly and invite international academic
   collaboration**, since this is pure science with no competitive
   urgency to keep closed.
4. **Treat it as a talent magnet** — a flagship question that draws
   planetary scientists to Indian institutions the way deep-space missions
   already do.

Ask: ISRO and DST to fund a long-horizon Mars-habitability research
programme within India's planetary-science portfolio.
