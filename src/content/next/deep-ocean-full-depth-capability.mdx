## Where I saw it

The deepest point in the ocean, the Mariana Trench's Challenger Deep at
roughly 11,000 metres, has only ever been reached by a handful of
individual crewed and uncrewed dives in all of human history — far fewer
descents than the number of people who have been to orbit. No nation
operates full-ocean-depth diving as a standing, repeatable capability;
each dive so far has been closer to a one-off expedition than
infrastructure.

## What's missing

Samudrayaan's Matsya-6000 submersible, already in development, targets
6,000 metres — a serious achievement that reaches most of the ocean floor
but not its deepest trenches. The opportunity beyond it is explicit: build
toward full-ocean-depth, repeatable diving capability, a feat genuinely no
country has achieved as a sustained programme.

## What India already has

- **The Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan (Matsya-6000)**, already
  building the crewed-submersible engineering base this would extend.
- **India's deep-sea pressure-vessel and materials research**, developed
  for the 6,000-metre submersible, directly relevant to a deeper design.
- **MoES's Blue Economy programmes**, an existing institutional home for
  extending the mission's ambition.

## The proposal

1. **Publish full-ocean-depth as the explicit next target**, once
   Matsya-6000 is proven, rather than treating 6,000 metres as the
   programme's ceiling.
2. **Invest early in pressure-hull materials research**, the single
   hardest engineering problem at trench depth, well before committing to
   a full design.
3. **Target repeatability, not a single record dive** — the goal is a
   standing capability, not a one-time expedition.
4. **Pair it with deep-trench science**, since the least-explored parts
   of the ocean are disproportionately in these deepest trenches.

Ask: the Ministry of Earth Sciences to publish a full-ocean-depth research
and engineering roadmap as the explicit successor to Samudrayaan's
6,000-metre Matsya mission.
