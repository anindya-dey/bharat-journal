## Where I saw it

Japan's Astroscale and Europe's ClearSpace are among the only companies in
the world actually flying missions to capture and de-orbit space debris,
or to refuel and repair ageing satellites in place. It's an emerging
capability almost no one has fielded yet — and an increasingly necessary
one as orbit fills up.

## What's missing

India operates a growing satellite fleet and launches on behalf of others,
but has no dedicated capability to service, refuel or de-orbit a satellite
once it's in space. As India's own orbital footprint grows, so does its
exposure to debris risk it currently has no way to mitigate directly.

## What India already has

- **ISRO's rendezvous and docking experiments (SpaDeX)**, already
  demonstrating the core technology on-orbit servicing requires.
- **A growing satellite-manufacturing and launch industry**, the natural
  customer base for a servicing capability.
- **IN-SPACe**, already positioned to license a private Indian servicing
  operator the way Japan and Europe did.

## The proposal

1. **Fund a debris-removal demonstrator mission**, building directly on
   SpaDeX's docking technology.
2. **A commercial satellite-servicing operator**, licensed through
   IN-SPACe, targeting both ISRO's fleet and the growing private satellite
   market.
3. **Contribute to international debris-tracking and removal norms**,
   positioning India as a rule-shaper in an area still being defined
   globally.
4. **Prioritise India's own crowded orbital slots first**, where the risk
   to its own assets is highest.

Ask: ISRO and IN-SPACe to fund a debris-removal demonstrator building on
the SpaDeX docking programme.
