## Where I saw it

The US's Next Generation Air Dominance programme, the UK-Italy-Japan
Global Combat Air Programme, and France-Germany-Spain's Future Combat Air
System are all designing sixth-generation fighters built around networked
sensor fusion and unmanned-aircraft teaming — years before their current
fifth-generation jets even enter service. The US Skyborg programme and
Australia's Loyal Wingman already fly unmanned combat drones alongside
crewed fighters today, and swarms of low-cost loitering munitions have
already proven decisive in recent conflicts. These aren't two separate
trends — a sixth-generation fighter *is*, by design, a crewed jet built to
work with unmanned wingmen and swarms.

## What's missing

India's AMCA programme is a genuine fifth-generation achievement still in
development, and CATS Warrior, India's own loyal-wingman unmanned
combat-drone concept, remains an early-stage programme relative to the
scale at which the US, China and Turkey are fielding unmanned combat
aircraft and swarms. Treated separately, India risks finishing AMCA only
to find the frontier has already moved past it — the same position it was
in with fourth-generation jets a generation ago — because the unmanned
half of next-generation air power was never funded at the same priority.

## What India already has

- **AMCA**, India's indigenous fifth-generation fighter programme, under
  development by DRDO's Aeronautical Development Agency and HAL.
- **CATS Warrior**, India's own loyal-wingman unmanned combat-drone
  concept, already in development, and DRDO's loitering-munition and
  swarm-drone research programmes, already underway.
- **A fast-growing domestic drone industry**, spurred by the 2021 Drone
  Rules opening Indian airspace to commercial and defence unmanned
  systems.
- **A deepening base of aerospace research institutions** across IITs
  and DRDO labs.

## The proposal

1. **A sixth-generation concept study**, run in parallel with AMCA
   development, not after it — focused on unmanned teaming, networked
   sensors and reduced pilot workload, built explicitly around CATS
   Warrior rather than treating it as a separate track.
2. **Fund CATS Warrior to a fielded squadron**, not a demonstrator, with
   a fixed in-service date, as the unmanned half of the same roadmap.
3. **A dedicated swarm-drone programme** — low-cost, mass-producible,
   AI-coordinated — distinct from the few, expensive loyal-wingman
   platforms, with its supply chain open to India's private drone
   startups so production scales at commercial-drone speed.
4. **Invest in the shared enabling technologies now** — AI mission
   systems, sensor fusion and directed-energy self-defence — so they're
   mature for both the crewed airframe and its unmanned wingmen when
   each programme needs them.
5. **Train for human-machine teaming** in the Air Force now, so doctrine
   is ready when the hardware is, and treat AMCA's production run as a
   bridge to this combined future, not an endpoint.

Ask: DRDO, ADA, HAL and the Ministry of Defence to fund AMCA's
sixth-generation successor study and CATS Warrior's path to squadron
service as one combined manned-unmanned air combat roadmap.
