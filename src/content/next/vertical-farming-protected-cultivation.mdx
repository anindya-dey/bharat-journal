## Where I saw it

The Netherlands exports more food by value than almost any country on
Earth, from a landmass smaller than many Indian states, by growing in
greenhouses that control water, light and temperature precisely — far
higher yield per acre and far less water per kilogram than open-field
farming. Singapore and Japan use the same principle at smaller scale to
grow fresh produce inside dense cities.

## What's missing

India's horticulture sector has scattered protected-cultivation pilots —
polyhouses and small greenhouse clusters — but nothing at the scale the
Netherlands model achieves, despite India facing even tighter water
constraints in many growing regions.

## What India already has

- **Existing polyhouse and greenhouse subsidy schemes**, under state
  horticulture missions, proving the model already works at pilot scale.
- **A large and growing domestic market for fresh, high-value produce** —
  near every major city, the exact customer base protected cultivation
  serves best.
- **India's solar and water-tech manufacturing base**, relevant to the
  climate-control systems greenhouses need.

## The proposal

1. **Greenhouse clusters near every major metro**, co-located with cold
   storage and direct-to-retail channels for high-value, perishable crops.
2. **Water-efficient climate control as the design default**, not an
   add-on, learning directly from the Netherlands' per-litre yield
   discipline.
3. **FPO-anchored financing**, so smallholders can access greenhouse
   technology collectively rather than needing individual capital.
4. **A domestic greenhouse-equipment manufacturing push**, so the
   technology is built in India, not entirely imported.

Ask: the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare to fund metro-adjacent
greenhouse clusters under the existing horticulture mission, at Netherlands
scale rather than pilot scale.
