## Where I saw it

Deep-sea polymetallic nodule mining and asteroid resource prospecting are
both still pre-commercial globally — a handful of companies and agencies
have tested prototype hardware, but no one operates a working autonomous
mining fleet in either environment yet. The two problems share a common
core: a robot that can operate for long periods, with minimal
communication lag tolerance, extracting material in an environment humans
cannot easily work in directly.

## What's missing

India's Deep Ocean Mission already targets seabed mineral exploration,
and the lunar ISRU proposal already targets resource extraction on the
Moon. What's missing is treating the underlying autonomous-mining
robotics as one shared research track, rather than two unconnected
efforts that each reinvent similar technology.

## What India already has

- **The Deep Ocean Mission's seabed exploration programme**, already
  targeting polymetallic nodules in India's exclusive economic zone.
- **The lunar ISRU resource-prospecting mission already proposed**,
  needing nearly identical remote-autonomy and extraction robotics.
- **DRDO and ISRO's robotics research programmes**, a shared talent base
  for both environments.

## The proposal

1. **A shared autonomous-extraction-robotics research programme**,
   explicitly serving both the Deep Ocean Mission and future lunar or
   asteroid resource work, rather than funding each separately.
2. **Start with seabed nodule-collection prototypes**, the nearer-term
   and better-funded of the two frontiers, and carry the autonomy
   research forward to space applications.
3. **Environmental safeguards built in from the start** for seabed
   mining, given the ecological risk of disturbing deep-sea ecosystems.
4. **Publish the shared-technology case explicitly**, so funding for one
   frontier is understood to accelerate the other.

Ask: the Ministry of Earth Sciences and ISRO to jointly fund a shared
autonomous-extraction-robotics research track serving both deep-sea and
future space resource missions.
