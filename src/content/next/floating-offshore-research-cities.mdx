## Where I saw it

Floating research and habitation platforms have been proposed and
piloted at small scale in several countries, but no nation has built a
permanently staffed floating research settlement at real scale — the
genuine counterpart, on water, to the underground research cities already
proposed in this journal for land. Between the two, nearly every extreme
environment on Earth would have a standing Indian research presence.

## What's missing

India's deep-ocean and marine-science programmes operate from land bases
and ships, with no permanent floating settlement positioned over its own
continental shelf — the same shelf the Deep Ocean Mission and offshore
aquaculture proposals already depend on being near.

## What India already has

- **The Deep Ocean Mission**, already building India's underwater
  research and engineering capability.
- **India's offshore oil-and-gas platform engineering experience**,
  directly transferable to a floating settlement's structural design.
- **A long continental shelf**, with calm-water stretches well suited to
  a permanently moored research platform.

## The proposal

1. **A feasibility study for one permanently staffed floating research
   platform**, anchored over the continental shelf, combining marine
   science, aquaculture research and renewable-energy testing.
2. **Build on offshore platform engineering already in India**, rather
   than importing floating-city concepts wholesale.
3. **Pair it with the offshore cage-aquaculture programme already
   proposed**, so the platform has an immediate operational purpose
   alongside pure research.
4. **Design for storm resilience from day one**, given the cyclone
   exposure of India's coastline.

Ask: the Ministry of Earth Sciences to commission a feasibility study for
a permanently staffed floating research platform on the continental
shelf.
