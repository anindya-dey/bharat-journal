## Where I saw it

Quantum sensing promises detection methods that classical radar and sonar
cannot match — potentially seeing through stealth coatings or detecting
submarines by their effect on quantum-entangled particles. No nation has
fielded a working quantum radar or sensor system yet; it remains an open
research race, which means an early, serious entrant can still shape where
the frontier lands.

## What's missing

India's National Quantum Mission funds computing, communication, sensing
and materials research broadly, but defence-specific quantum sensing — the
application with arguably the most strategic payoff — isn't yet a
distinct, resourced track within it.

## What India already has

- **The National Quantum Mission**, already funding quantum sensing
  research among its pillars.
- **DRDO and DST quantum labs**, with active atomic-clock and
  quantum-sensing research programmes.
- **A growing base of quantum physicists** at IISc, IITs and TIFR.

## The proposal

1. **A defence-quantum-sensing track** within the National Quantum
   Mission, resourced separately from civilian applications.
2. **Target submarine and stealth detection first**, the two applications
   with the clearest military payoff.
3. **Joint DRDO-DST quantum labs**, so defence research benefits directly
   from the civilian mission's talent pipeline rather than duplicating it.
4. **Publish openly on dual-use applications**, reserving only the
   genuinely sensitive work for classified channels, to keep India's
   academic quantum community engaged.

Ask: DRDO and DST to establish a jointly-funded defence-quantum-sensing
track within the National Quantum Mission.
