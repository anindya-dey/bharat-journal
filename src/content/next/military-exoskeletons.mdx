## Where I saw it

US military research into powered exosuits aims to let infantry carry
heavier loads over longer distances with less fatigue and fewer
musculoskeletal injuries — gear-laden soldiers are often limited more by
the body than the mission. No country has fielded a combat exosuit at
scale yet; it remains an emerging capability.

## What's missing

Indian troops deployed at extreme altitude carry some of the heaviest
loads, in some of the harshest cold, of any army in the world, for
resupply and patrol alike. This is precisely the use case where even a
modest, load-bearing exoskeleton — not a full combat suit — would deliver
an outsized benefit well before the technology matures for broader combat
use.

## What India already has

- **DRDO's robotics and biomechanics research programmes**, a starting
  base for exosuit development.
- **Decades of high-altitude logistics and cold-weather-gear experience**,
  from the world's highest battlefield infrastructure.
- **India's growing robotics and advanced-materials research base** across
  IITs and DRDO labs.

## The proposal

1. **A load-bearing exoskeleton pilot** for high-altitude resupply and
   patrol troops, focused on reducing injury and fatigue rather than full
   combat augmentation.
2. **Cold-weather power and battery research** as a distinct track, since
   extreme-altitude power density is a harder problem than temperate-
   climate exosuit designs solve for.
3. **Partner with India's prosthetics and biomechanics research
   institutions**, which already work on adjacent human-augmentation
   problems.
4. **Field-test before scaling**, with troops already deployed at extreme
   altitude, rather than lab-only development.

Ask: DRDO to commission a load-bearing exoskeleton pilot for high-altitude
infantry units.
