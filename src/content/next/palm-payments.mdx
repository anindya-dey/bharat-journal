## The Spark

In China, shoppers hold a palm over a sensor and walk out — no phone, no card.
Amazon One runs the same trick in US stores. The future of payments looks less
like an app and more like a handshake.

## The Gap — and the correction

The lazy version of this idea is "India should go cashless like China."
India doesn't need to catch up on cashlessness — as the UPI chart shows,
India's public rail already processes more real-time transactions than
anyone, ~84% of the country's digital payments, at zero merchant cost.
The actual gaps are narrower and more interesting:

- **Feature-phone and no-phone users** — UPI 123PAY and voice payments
  reach them, but the experience is clunkier than a palm.
- **Deliberate speed** — a palm or fingerprint payment is faster than
  any phone flow, useful exactly where queues are longest: transit gates,
  canteens, stadium concessions, tolls.
- **The unbanked visitor** — tourists can't join UPI without an Indian
  account; biometric prepaid could let a visitor pay like a local.

## The Precedent

India has run **AePS — Aadhaar-enabled Payment System** — for a decade:
fingerprint authentication for bank transactions at micro-ATMs, used
billions of times in rural India. Palm-vein and face biometrics are
evolutions of something that already exists in production, not an
import. China's palm systems are closed, proprietary ecosystems; India's
version should be a standard any bank can implement on NPCI rails.

## The Proposal

Ask NPCI/RBI to run a **standards pilot for biometric-present payments**:

1. Palm/finger/face as an *option* alongside QR — never the only way in
   (privacy by choice, not mandate).
2. Prepaid instruments (like UPI One World) that a visitor or
   feature-phone user can top up and spend palm-first.
3. Privacy guardrails explicit from day one: templates stored only by
   the certified provider, revocable, never transaction-linked to
   Aadhaar's welfare uses.
4. First venues: metro gates, IRCTC catering counters, stadium and
   temple queue management — the exact places queues are measured in
   minutes.
