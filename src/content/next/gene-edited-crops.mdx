## Where I saw it

CRISPR-based gene editing is being used worldwide to create crops that
tolerate drought, flooding and heat without introducing genes from another
species — a technical and regulatory distinction that has let several
countries move faster on this than on older transgenic crops. India's own
2022 guidelines already carve out a lighter regulatory path for this exact
category of editing.

## What's missing

India has the regulatory framework and the agricultural-research base, but
a fast-tracked pipeline of gene-edited staple varieties — rice, wheat and
pulses bred for the droughts and flooding climate change is making more
frequent — has not yet reached farmers at scale.

## What India already has

- **India's 2022 SDN-1/SDN-2 gene-editing guidelines**, which already
  exempt certain gene-edited crops without foreign DNA from the stricter
  GM regulatory path.
- **ICAR's extensive crop-breeding research network**, with decades of
  work on drought- and flood-tolerant varieties through conventional
  breeding.
- **A genuine climate case** — recurring droughts and floods already
  affecting staple-crop yields.

## The proposal

1. **A fast-tracked gene-editing pipeline for staple crops**, using the
   2022 guidelines' lighter regulatory path, targeting rice and wheat
   first.
2. **Public-sector-led development**, so the resulting varieties are
   affordable and not locked behind a single company's licensing terms.
3. **Transparent public communication** about what gene editing is and
   isn't, learning from the public-trust challenges transgenic crops faced
   earlier.
4. **Field trials in the most climate-exposed districts first**, so the
   benefit reaches the farmers who need it most, soonest.

Ask: ICAR and the Department of Biotechnology to publish a fast-tracked
gene-edited staple-crop pipeline using the existing 2022 guidelines.
