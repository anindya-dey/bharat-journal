## Where I saw it

Singapore's hawker centers: 100+ NEA-managed centers, ~6,000 stalls,
fixed subsidised rent, hygiene grading on every stall, and — the
part people miss — **a UNESCO-listed intangible cultural heritage**
inscription (2020). Street food, given infrastructure and dignity,
became the country's most beloved institution.

## What's missing

India's street food is already world-class — Indore's Chappan Dukan,
Ahmedabad's Manek Chowk, and Amritsar's food lanes are legendary in their own
right. The opportunity is the layer Singapore perfected on top of that
talent: a predictable hygiene standard, stable licensing that lets vendors
invest in their stalls, planned placement near monuments, and a tourism
system that markets the whole trail. The street-food economy is enormous —
and a management layer lets more of that value reach both the vendor and the
visitor.

## What India already has

- **Singapore** — relocation of street vendors into managed centers
  began in the 1970s; NEA runs rent subsidies, grading, and
  training today.
- **India's own PM SVANidhi** — vending-cart loans during COVID
  showed that hawkers respond to dignified formalisation.
- **Night bazaars** in Shillong, food streets under [Smart Cities](https://smartcities.gov.in/)
  (e.g., Ujjain's Mahakal Lok approach) — fragments exist.

## The proposal

1. **Tourism-district food hubs first** — managed, roofed, graded
   food hubs at 100 high-footfall monument districts (Agra, Puri,
   Tirupati, Shirdi, Ajmer...) with: stall licensing, water/waste,
   hygiene grading displayed (FSSAI), and rent set low enough that
   the incumbent street vendor moves *in*, not out.
2. **District-HQ rollout** — one managed food hub per district
   headquarters (aligning with ODOP's district-unit logic):
   the hub features the district's ODOP food products.
3. **Vendor economics** — licences to existing vendors first
   (not big-capital food courts), small fixed rent, digital
   payments via [UPI](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi), and the passport/tourist-card as the
   marketing hook.
4. **The brand** — a national "Street Food Trails" certification,
   grade cards displayed like Singapore's A/B/D plates.

Ask: Ministry of Tourism (site selection) + Ministry of Housing &
Urban Affairs (hub infrastructure) + FSSAI (grading) — pilot in
the 10 mural-trail cities for compounding tourism effect.
