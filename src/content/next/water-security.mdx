## Where I saw it

Water is the constraint beneath every other ambition — food, cities,
industry, energy. India receives enormous rainfall, but in the wrong places
and the wrong seasons. The civilisational answer has always been to move and
store it.

## What's missing

India's per-capita water availability is falling as demand rises. The
opportunity is a layered strategy: move water where it's needed, reuse what
cities already use, and tap the sea for coastal metros — so that water stops
being the ceiling on growth.

## What India already has

- **Ken–Betwa link** — the first river-interlinking project under
  construction, joining two basins.
- **[Jal Jeevan Mission](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/)** — piped water to rural households at national scale.
- **Large dams and canal systems** — from Bhakra-Nangal onward, India has
  decades of water-infrastructure experience.

## The proposal

1. **Accelerate river interlinking** with rigorous environmental and
   resettlement safeguards, starting with the projects already approved.
2. **Water recycling at scale** — treated wastewater for industry and
   irrigation, freeing fresh water for drinking.
3. **Coastal desalination** for the biggest metros, powered by renewables, to
   drought-proof urban supply.
4. **A national water data layer** — real-time monitoring of rivers,
   reservoirs and groundwater, so allocation is managed with data, not
   guesswork.

Ask: the Ministry of Jal Shakti to publish an integrated water-security road
map combining interlinking, recycling and desalination.
