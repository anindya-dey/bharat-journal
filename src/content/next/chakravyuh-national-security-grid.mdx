## Where I saw it

"Chakravyuh" — the multi-ringed, nearly inescapable battle formation from
the Mahabharata — has already entered Indian public life as the informal
name for layered defence: it's how media and military commentary describe
Mission Sudarshan Chakra's air-defence rings, and it has been borrowed
for everything from border-technology explainers to exam-security
operations. The name has stuck because the idea behind it is sound: a
threat that breaks through one ring should meet another, in every domain
that matters, not just the sky.

## What's missing

India already runs several real, separately-built layered-security
systems — an air-defence shield, a border sensor grid, a coastal watch —
each under its own name, budget and ministry, with no single doctrine
binding them together or extending the same logic to every domain that
needs it: space, the undersea, critical economic corridors, heritage
sites, the underground vaults and research cities already proposed in
this journal, and the capital cities — and satellite capitals — that
everything else protects.

## What India already has

- **Mission Sudarshan Chakra**, India's own named, in-progress, AI-driven
  layered air-defence shield — already covered as its own proposal in
  this journal.
- **CIBMS (the Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System)**,
  the Ministry of Home Affairs's sensor, radar and command-and-control
  grid for the land border, already piloted and expanding.
- **The Coastal Security Scheme**, a multi-phase Ministry of Home Affairs
  and Indian Coast Guard programme already watching India's coastline.
- **CISF's dedicated monument-security mandate**, already guarding the
  Taj Mahal and a growing list of other heritage sites.
- **Every other layer this doctrine would bind together, already
  proposed separately in this journal** — a tri-service space command,
  a counter-space interceptor fleet and space-situational-awareness
  network for orbit, unmanned underwater vehicles and undersea
  cable-repair drones for the deep, a national cyber-security grid for
  digital infrastructure, a hardened continuity-of-government site and
  the national vault network for what must never be lost, and satellite
  capitals for distributing the load the capital itself carries.

## The proposal

**Chakravyuh isn't a new system to build from scratch — it's the name and
doctrine that binds the systems already being built.**

1. **Adopt "Chakravyuh" as the public doctrine name** for India's
   full-spectrum layered security posture, giving citizens and planners
   one mental model instead of a dozen unconnected acronyms.
2. **One coordinating body — the National Security Council Secretariat —
   to track every layer against a single doctrine**: air (Mission
   Sudarshan Chakra), land border (CIBMS), coastline (Coastal Security
   Scheme), space (the tri-service command and counter-space proposals),
   the undersea (the UUV and cable-repair proposals), economic corridors
   (the defence-manufacturing corridors and strategic-resource-
   independence proposals), cyberspace (the national cyber-security
   grid), the underground (the vault network and research cities), and
   governance continuity (the hardened bunker proposal).
3. **Extend the same sensor-and-command-fusion model used for air
   defence to the domains that don't have it yet** — starting with
   economic corridors and heritage sites, which currently rely on
   point-security rather than a networked layer.
4. **Publish one annual public readiness report** across every layer,
   the way individual missions already publish their own progress, so
   the doctrine is accountable as a whole, not just as scattered parts.

Ask: the National Security Council Secretariat to formally adopt a
Chakravyuh doctrine binding every layered-security proposal already in
motion or already proposed, rather than letting each grow independently.
