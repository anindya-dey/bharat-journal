## Where I saw it

The US Skyborg programme and Australia's Loyal Wingman fly unmanned combat
drones alongside crewed fighters, taking on the riskiest parts of a mission.
Turkey's drone fleets, and swarms of low-cost loitering munitions, have
already proven decisive in recent conflicts — air power is no longer solely
about expensive crewed platforms.

## What's missing

India has started building toward this with its own combat-drone-teaming
concept, but it remains an early-stage programme relative to the scale at
which the US, China and Turkey are fielding unmanned combat aircraft and
swarms. The opportunity is to treat this as a first-tier priority alongside
crewed fighter programmes, not a secondary one.

## What India already has

- **CATS Warrior**, India's own loyal-wingman unmanned combat-drone concept,
  already in development.
- **A fast-growing domestic drone industry**, spurred by the 2021 Drone
  Rules opening Indian airspace to commercial and defence unmanned systems.
- **DRDO's loitering-munition and swarm-drone research programmes**,
  already underway.

## The proposal

1. **Fund CATS Warrior to a fielded squadron**, not a demonstrator, with a
   fixed in-service date.
2. **A dedicated swarm-drone programme** — low-cost, mass-producible,
   AI-coordinated — distinct from the few, expensive loyal-wingman
   platforms.
3. **Open the supply chain to India's private drone startups**, so
   production scales at commercial-drone speed, not defence-procurement
   speed.
4. **Train for human-machine teaming** in the Air Force and Army aviation
   corps now, so doctrine is ready when the hardware is.

Ask: DRDO, HAL and the Ministry of Defence to commit CATS Warrior to a
funded, dated path to squadron service.
