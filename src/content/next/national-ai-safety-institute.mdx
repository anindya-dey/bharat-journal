## Where I saw it

The UK, US, EU and Japan have established AI safety institutes to test frontier
models and set standards. India runs some of the world's largest AI-powered
systems — payments, welfare, identity — and should help define how AI is made
safe at population scale.

## What's missing

India is deploying AI faster than it is governing it. The opportunity is to
build a public capability for evaluation and assurance *before* the technology
is woven deeper into critical systems — turning India's scale into a source of
global standards, not just deployment.

## What India already has

- **India's AI Mission** and the **AI Safety Institute** discussions are
  underway internationally.
- **[Aadhaar](https://uidai.gov.in/), [UPI](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi) and [CoWIN](https://www.cowin.gov.in/)** show India can run critical digital systems with
  strong governance.
- **[Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/) and Indian-language models** give India a stake in sovereign AI.

## The proposal

1. **A National AI Safety Institute** — independent evaluation, red-teaming and
   certification of AI models and systems, especially for public use.
2. **Sector standards** — safety and accuracy benchmarks for AI in health,
   finance, education and welfare, where errors have real consequences.
3. **A public AI register** — transparency about where AI is used in government
   services, and how it is audited.
4. **Global cooperation** — link with partner safety institutes so India helps
   shape international AI norms.

Ask: MeitY and the National AI Mission to establish a National AI Safety
Institute as public infrastructure.
